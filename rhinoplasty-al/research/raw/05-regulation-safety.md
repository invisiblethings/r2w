# Albania: regulation and safety of cosmetic surgery / rhinoplasty

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: WebSearch (standard) and WebFetch, Albanian and English. Where a page dated itself only through a site banner I used the article's own timestamp. "Unverified" means I could not confirm it from a primary page I opened.
Note: Albanian-language news (Shqiptarja, BalkanWeb) was fetched and read directly. Telegrafi returned HTTP 402 on fetch, so Telegrafi items are known only from search snippets (marked).

## 1. Who licenses and accredits; is there a public register?

### Findings
- **Licensing basis.** Law 10 107 / 30.03.2009 "Për kujdesin shëndetësor" art. 27 makes licensing and re-licensing mandatory for anyone providing health services. Art. 38 sets a fine of 500,000 lek for violating art. 27 (5x on repeat, plus referral to prosecutors). Source: ISHSH letter to Faktoje, 31 Jan 2024: https://faktoje.al/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Kthim-pergjigje-Esmeralda-Keta-Faktoje.pdf (read via pdftotext).
- **Licence categories.** CoM Decision (VKM) 538 / 26.05.2009, consolidated and updated text on QKB site (amendments to 853/26.12.2024): https://qkb.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/VKM-538-26-05-2009perditesuar.pdf (I read it).
  - Category II.6. Subcategory **II.6.A** covers primary medical services. Code **A.4** is a "Qendër mjekësore" with several specialties, outpatient care and "mikrokirurgjikale" treatments. Code **A.5** includes "qendra estetike ku afrohet shërbim mjekësor" (aesthetic centres that offer a medical service).
  - Subcategory **II.6.B** covers hospital services: B.1 hospital, B.2 day hospital. Licences have no expiry.
  - For II.6.B the deciding institution is the Ministry of Health, via a "Komisioni i Shqyrtimit të Kërkesave për Licenca dhe/ose Certifikim për Shërbimet Shëndetësore"; the stated deadline is 30 days. A hospital needs a full-time General Medical Director. (VKM 538, text on II.6.B.)
  - So a private rhinoplasty centre could be licensed as a day hospital (II.6.B.2) or hospital (II.6.B.1). Which code a given clinic holds is unverified.
- **Who issues what (2026).** A Ministry of Health request-register entry (11.02.2026) says that for dental licences II.6.A.3 and II.6.A.1 the institution that examines and decides is **Qendra Kombëtare e Biznesit (QKB)**, citing VKM 538. Source: https://shendetesia.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Regjister-2026.pdf (pdftotext). The old "QKL" was merged into QKB under Law 131/2015 (Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Business_Center_(Albania); secondary source). I did not confirm which body issues II.6.A.4/A.5 and II.6.B licences in practice (the VKM text names the Ministry for B). Treat who-issues-which as unverified beyond the VKM text.
- **ISHSH does NOT license.** ISHSH states its competences "nuk përfshijnë licensimin e asnjë lloj veprimtarie apo subjekti"; it inspects under Law 10 433/2011 and plans inspections from its own database or QKB data (same Faktoje letter, 31.01.2024). ISHSH site: https://ins-shendetesor.gov.al/ (I opened it; it has legislation, reports, open-data, inspection and accreditation checklists, and a "Denonco" contact channel at https://ins-shendetesor.gov.al/kontakt/). Its open-data page lists only: inspection programmes (ZIP), annual reports, quarterly reporting (https://ins-shendetesor.gov.al/opendata/). I did not open the ZIP. No list of inspected or licensed entities was visible.
- **Accreditation.** VKM 865 / 24.12.2019 sets the accreditation process; ISHSH inspection groups check base standards and report to the Ministry and "institucioni përgjegjës për akreditimin" (same letter). The 2019 health-care law linked accreditation and licensing: failing base standards can mean suspension and licence revocation (Shqiptarja, 8 May 2019: https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/miratohet-ligji-i-ri-i-kujdesit-shendetesor-manastirliu-auditim-klinik-spitaleve-per-trajtimin-e-pacienteve). Whether "QKCSA" still exists as a named body: **unverified**. Neither the VKM nor the ISHSH letter names it. Its website did not resolve (qkcsa.gov.al: DNS failure).
- **ISHSH 2026 stance.** Chief Inspector Luneda Sufali (Shqiptarja, 11 Mar 2026): in 2026 priority goes to inspections of basic standards in non-public institutions as part of the initial accreditation phase, and to "disciplining" licensing of clinics and medical offices doing invasive diagnostics and surgery. The article says 73 public and private hospitals operate. It also says a citizen reporting portal was planned (the spelling given is either Sigurt.gov.al or Siguri.gov.al). https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/licencimi-inspektoratit-shteteror-shendetesor-kontrolle-me-te-forta-per-spitalet-private-ne-fokus-kirurgjia-plastike-nga-prilli-denoncime-dhe-online
- **ISHSH activity figures.** A Shqiptarja piece (date not shown on the page; likely 2026, **unverified**) reports for 2025: 4,993 inspections (4,882 regular, 111 re-inspections), 13 fines, 11 interim decisions for urgent measures, 296 warnings. No mention of aesthetic clinics. https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/alarm-nga-inspektimet-mbetjet-spitalore-ruheshin-ne-tualete-dhe-korridore
  - The ISHSH letter says it did 62 inspections and re-inspections in 2023 for quality, safety and accreditation standards.

Practical check method: search the clinic's legal name or NIPT in the QKB entity search (or the Atlas mirror), look for a licence of category II.6 (A.4/A.5 or B.2/B.1) marked active, and cross-check that the operating surgeon is a member of the Order of Physicians.

## 2. Order of Physicians (Urdhri i Mjekëve)

- Law 123/2014 on the Order of Physicians: nobody may practise medicine without Order membership and an individual practice licence; the Order issues and revokes the licence and keeps a "Regjistri Themeltar" (founding register) (consolidated PDF: https://www.qsut.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ligj-2014-09-25-123-Per-Urdhrin-e-Mjekesve-ne-RSH.pdf; found via search, content seen only as search summary; **article numbers unverified**). Amended by Normative Act no. 2 of 1.08.2024 (https://aksesdrejtesi.al/dokumenta/1724919824akt-normativ-2024-08-01-2.pdf); later consolidated as Law 94/2024 per a search summary (**unverified**). Constitutional Court decision no. 58 / 15.10.2025 upheld ministry involvement in the Order's disciplinary commissions and ethics-code approval (https://www.gjykatakushtetuese.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/vend.5825.pdf; search summary only).
- **Searchable public register: not found.** I could not find the Order's official website. Candidate domains tested failed DNS. Three searches returned only legislation. Conclusion: a public online lookup of doctors/specialists is **unverified / not located**. The Ministry portal https://mjeke.shendetesia.gov.al ("Mjekë për Shqipërinë") is a recruitment/vacancy platform for public posts, not a register (I opened it). Practical route: ask the Order directly for confirmation of licence and specialty (plastic surgery vs other).
- Order chair named in 2024 press as Fatmir Brahimaj/Ibrahimaj (spelling varies: Fortune 2024 gives "Fatmir Ibrahimaj"; undated Shqiptarja article gives "Brahimaj").

## 3. Laws and rules on aesthetic surgery / centres

- No dedicated "law on aesthetic surgery" found. Rules come from: Law 10 107/2009 (health care; amended by 51/2013, 76/2015, 27/2019 and 99/2024 of 12.9.2024, per search summary, consolidated PDF: https://www.qsut.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Ligj-Nr.10-107-date-30.3.2009-Per-Kujdesin-Shendetesor-1.pdf); Law 10 081/2009 on licences and VKM 538 (aesthetic centres under II.6.A.5); hospital service law (Law 55/2022, https://www.qsut.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ligj-2022-07-07-55-Per-sherbimin-Spitalor.pdf; not read); Law 123/2014 on the Order.
- Who may perform: a physician must hold a specialisation diploma and an Order licence (dermatologist Erjona Shehu, and UMSH vice-president Dritan Trepça, in the Fiks Fare investigation: Shqiptarja, 4 May 2022, https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/ivestigimi-nw-shqipwri-botoks-dhe-filler-ne-parukeri-nga-mashtrues-pa-diploma). These are interview statements, not legal citations. Whether "plastic surgeon" is a protected specialty title enforced against others: **unverified**.
- Press claims of a "2017 law" for the cosmetic sector that doctors call "very unclear" come from AFP-derived coverage (via Telegrafi snippet; AFP 2021 piece also at https://exit.al/en/plastic-surgery-boom-in-albania-during-pandemic, which itself does not mention a law). I could not identify the instrument: **unverified**.
- A Shqiptarja piece (13 Aug 2023) says "no specific laws or precise rules" exist for aesthetic surgery, attributed to unnamed operators and unsourced (https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/biznesi-i-bukurise-tendenca-po-shkon-drejt-natyrale-heqja-e-dhjamit-dhe-hunda-mbeten-te-popullarizuara). Treat as opinion.
- Botox (botulinum toxin) products: the Gremi case reports (below) say the product ("Butox") is not authorised for sale in Albania; Fortune/AFP (31 Oct 2024) says Botox is "prohibited in Albania" - I did not verify the legal basis.
- 2020-2026 changes found: Law 55/2022 (hospitals), amendments to Law 10 107 (2024), VKM 538 amendments of 2020, 2021, 2024. No aesthetic-specific change found. ISHSH's 2026 plan targets plastic-surgery licensing (see §1).
- **Medical advertising rules: not found.** Searches returned only the Order's Code of Ethics (2011): https://mjeke.shendetesia.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Kodi-i-Etikes-dhe-Deontologjise-Mjekesore-2011.pdf (I did not read advertising provisions; **unverified**).

## 5. Statistics on medical / aesthetic tourism

- **INSTAT** foreign arrivals by purpose, "Health treatment" line (monthly press release, opened and parsed): January-October 2025 = **327** vs 603 in Jan-Oct 2024 (-45.8%), out of 11,137,331 foreign arrivals. https://www.instat.gov.al/media/s3tos3rb/movements-of-citizens-in-albania-october-2025.pdf . Earlier annual values quoted by a search summary of INSTAT releases (not opened by me, **unverified**): 2021 1,504; 2022 2,392; 2023 949; 2024 637. Category is the stated trip purpose at the border, so it undercounts. There is no INSTAT breakdown for cosmetic or dental.
- Secondary: Tirana Times citing INSTAT for 2012 (1,188) and 2017 (633): https://www.tiranatimes.com/more-visiting-albania-on-health-tourism-purposes_115057/ (page would not load; search summary only, **unverified**).
- **Fortune/AFP, 31 Oct 2024:** sector estimated at EUR 200-250 million a year; "at least 50,000 Italians" travel to Tirana for treatment yearly; one dermatologist reports 750-1,000 foreign patients a year; prosecutors inspected 30 cosmetic clinics that month. These are estimates, not official statistics. https://fortune.com/europe/2024/10/31/albania-rising-star-europe-medical-tourism/
- Clinic self-reports (not independent): Da Vinci clinic says 60% of customers foreign, aesthetic surgeries +23% in Jan-Jul 2022, local clinics report ~30% rise in demand (Exit.al, 25 Aug 2022: https://exit.al/en/foreigners-pick-albania-for-plastic-surgery-in-2022); a clinic founder claims ~1,500 patients/year and "thousands" of French, Italian, English and Swiss patients (Shqiptarja reprinting Forbes France, 8 Oct 2018: https://shqiptarja.com/lajm/forbes-tirana-destinacioni-me-i-sigurt-ne-europe-tani-edhe-lider-i-turizmit-mjekesor); KEIT clinic claims 15,000+ procedures in 12 years (BalkanWeb, 13 May 2023: https://www.balkanweb.com/?p=2169086). All marketing claims.
- No Ministry of Health statistic on foreign aesthetic patients found.
