Revision rhinoplasty, also called secondary rhinoplasty, is surgery to correct problems after a first nose operation. The problem may be how the nose looks, how it works, or both. It is more common than people expect, and it is usually a harder operation than the first one.

This guide covers why revisions happen, how long to wait, how to find a surgeon, and what revisions cost in Albania.

## What is revision rhinoplasty?

Revision rhinoplasty is an operation that corrects problems left by an earlier rhinoplasty. Cleveland Clinic defines secondary (revision) rhinoplasty this way and says these procedures are often more complex to repair ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11011-rhinoplasty)).

It can be small, such as smoothing an irregularity, or large, such as rebuilding support with cartilage grafts. Your surgeon will decide which depends on what was done before and what tissue remains.

## Why do people need a revision?

People need revisions for aesthetic problems, breathing problems, or both. Cleveland Clinic says about 15% of rhinoplasty patients need a revision to make small changes. ASPS lists the possibility of revisional surgery as a risk of rhinoplasty.

In a 2010 prospective study of 104 patients seeking revision, tip asymmetry was the most common aesthetic complaint of both patients and surgeons, followed by a crooked middle third and an irregular upper third. The most bothersome complaints included difficulty breathing. Of 64 patients who reported nasal obstruction, 94% had objective findings that matched ([HealthDay summary](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/ear-nose-and-throat/tip-asymmetry-most-common-reason-for-rhinoplasty-643388.html)). In other words, a breathing complaint after surgery usually has a physical cause and deserves a proper examination.

A 2021 retrospective study from one team reviewed 252 revisions after open rhinoplasty, a revision rate of 10.8%. The top reasons were insufficient tip rotation (37.7%), hanging columella (30.2%) and supratip deformity (28.6%). Technique choices affected risk: strut grafts raised the odds of inadequate tip rotation about 5.3-fold compared with the tongue-in-groove technique ([Plast Reconstr Surg 2021](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/20149738-f949-4049-957e-6c424cd28a83/revision-rhinoplasty-after-open-rhinoplasty-lessons-from-252-cases-and-analysis-of-risk-factors)). These are one team's figures, not a universal rate.

## How long should you wait before revision?

Wait until the nose has healed, which usually takes about a year. Cleveland Clinic says the final result usually appears after about a year, that minor swelling can persist until then, and suggests waiting until the nose has fully healed before revision. A second Cleveland Clinic page gives 12 to 18 months for the last swelling to resolve.

We did not find an opened source with a firm revision-specific waiting period, so treat "about a year" as a general rule, not a guarantee. Some problems may not be able to wait. A blocked airway, pain, signs of infection or a visible structural change should be seen by a surgeon soon. Judging the tip in the first weeks is rarely useful because swelling hides the result. See swelling month by month.

## Who should do a revision?

Choose a surgeon who performs revisions regularly and can explain what went wrong. Cleveland Clinic offers to revise procedures done elsewhere and offers second opinions. A good second opinion starts with your records: the operative report, if you can get it, and photographs from before and after.

If your first operation was abroad and the surgeon will not see you, ask a surgeon near home for an examination and see what follow-up is possible. Our guide on [problems after you get home](/safety/problems-after-you-get-home/) explains the steps.

## Is revision rhinoplasty right for you?

It depends on whether surgery can fix the problem you notice. Cleveland Clinic's general criteria apply: you should be healthy, not smoke, and have realistic goals. A surgeon may recommend waiting, a smaller correction, or no surgery. If you are repeatedly unhappy with changes others cannot see, you may find it helpful to talk with your doctor about whether body dysmorphic disorder, a treatable condition affecting how people see their appearance, is part of the picture. That is not a judgement. It is a reason to get support before another operation.

## How is it done and what is recovery like?

The surgeon plans around the existing scar tissue and what the first operation removed or added. Open and closed approaches are both used; one closed-rhinoplasty paper lists revisions involving limited dorsal augmentation as suitable for closed surgery, while larger rebuilding usually needs open exposure and grafts. See [open rhinoplasty](/procedures/open-rhinoplasty/).

Recovery follows the general pattern. Cleveland Clinic lists a splint for one to two weeks, return to work or school after one to two weeks, swelling and bruising peaking at four to six weeks, and about 90% of swelling gone at three months. Expect your own timeline to be longer if grafts were needed.

## What are the risks?

The risks are those of any rhinoplasty, and the chance of a result that still falls short is higher when tissue has already been altered. ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, septal perforation, poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance and revision surgery. See [risks and complications](/safety/risks-and-complications/).

## What does revision cost in Albania?

Revision is priced above primary surgery. One Tirana private hospital publishes €3,800 to €5,500 for revision, compared with €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty (own site, undated; hotel and flights extra) ([price page](https://hygeia.al/plastic-surgery/facial-surgery/rhinoplasty-albania/)). Ask in writing what happens if you need a further correction: whether the surgeon charges, whether you pay for the operating theatre, and whether you need to travel again. See [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/).
