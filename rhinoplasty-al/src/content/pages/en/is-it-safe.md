Rhinoplasty in Albania can be safe, and it can be unsafe. The country is not the deciding factor. The facility, the anaesthesia and the surgeon are. What is different in Albania is how hard those things are to check from outside. This guide sets out what the law requires, what you can and cannot verify, and the checks to make before you book.

## Is rhinoplasty safe anywhere?

No surgery is risk-free, and rhinoplasty carries the risks described in [risks and complications](/safety/risks-and-complications/). The NHS says "no surgery is risk-free" and that complications can happen abroad or in the UK. MedlinePlus lists general risks such as reactions to medicines, breathing problems, bleeding, infection and bruising, and procedure-specific risks such as loss of nose support, contour deformities, worsened nasal breathing and the need for more surgery.

Travelling adds its own issues. The NHS says safety standards abroad may be lower and overseas clinics may not provide follow-up to the same standard. ASPS adds that surgeon training and credentials can be hard to verify abroad, and that some destinations lack formal accreditation boards for physicians or facilities.

## What does Albanian law require?

Anyone providing health services in Albania must hold a licence. Law 10 107/2009 on health care, article 27, makes licensing and re-licensing mandatory, and article 38 sets a fine of 500,000 lek for breaking article 27. That is stated in a reply from the State Health Inspectorate (ISHSH), published on 31 January 2024.

The categories are set by decision of the Council of Ministers (VKM) 538/2009. Under category II.6, the types relevant to surgery are:

| Licence | What it covers |
|---|---|
| A.4 | Medical centre with micro-surgery |
| A.5 | Aesthetic centre offering a medical service |
| B.1 | Hospital |
| B.2 | Day hospital |

For categories B, the decision belongs to the Ministry of Health. In our reading of the VKM, a rhinoplasty under general anaesthesia belongs in a hospital (B.1) or day hospital (B.2) licence. If a clinic holds an aesthetic centre (A.5) licence, ask which licence covers the room where you will be operated on. This is our interpretation, not a legal opinion. Ask the clinic to tell you the licence category in writing.

Doctors are regulated separately. Law 123/2014 requires membership of the Order of Physicians and an individual licence.

## What can you check yourself?

You can check three things, and one of them is not possible online.

1. **The clinic's licence.** Search the clinic's legal name or tax number (NIPT) in the QKB entity search and look for an active category II.6 licence. A non-official mirror site shows licence status per business. If the clinic cannot give you its legal name and NIPT, treat that as a warning. See [red flags and unlicensed clinics](/safety/red-flags-unlicensed-clinics/).
2. **The surgeon's licence.** The Order of Physicians keeps the register, but we found no public online search. Ask the surgeon for the Order licence number and specialist diploma, and ask the Order to confirm them in writing. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).
3. **A foreign surgeon's home register.** For Italian surgeons this is the FNOMCeO register; for Turkish surgeons, the Ministry of Health or the plastic surgery association. A foreign surgeon also needs authorisation to practise in Albania.

We found no public list of accredited institutions in Albania either.

## What is the regulator doing?

The inspectorate says it is paying attention. In March 2026, ISHSH's chief inspector said, as reported by Shqiptarja, that licensing of clinics and offices doing invasive diagnostics and surgery was a 2026 priority, with plastic surgery in focus and a citizen reporting portal planned. That shows the problem is recognised. It does not mean the problem is solved.

ISHSH also has a record of acting on unlicensed premises. In October 2023 it fined a dental clinic in Tirana 500,000 lek for operating without a licence, an administrative finding documented in its own letter. That was not a cosmetic clinic, and we mention it only to show the rule is enforced in at least some cases.

## What else makes surgery safer?

Beyond the paperwork, the sources give a short list.

- **Two consultations with the surgeon.** The NHS says you should have two before surgery, and to avoid consultations that involve only sales staff.
- **Before-and-after pictures** of the surgeon's own work.
- **A language you understand**, so the surgeon can explain risks and answer your questions.
- **Insurance.** The NHS says travel insurance normally does not cover problems arising from surgery. Check what the clinic's insurance covers and plan how you would get home in an emergency.
- **Follow-up.** Ask what happens if complications occur, and where follow-up takes place. Read [aftercare once you are home](/recovery/aftercare-at-home/).
- **Anaesthesia and facility.** ASPS says safe surgery requires proper anaesthesia, sterile technique, modern equipment and trained surgeons.
