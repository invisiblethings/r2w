The setting matters as much as the surgeon, because a rhinoplasty is usually done under general anaesthetic and you need to be watched afterwards. In Albania the word "clinic" covers very different things, from a university-style hospital to a beauty centre, so you need to check which licence the place holds.

This guide explains the categories, what each can legally do, and what to ask. It does not rank any facility; for method see [how we rank clinics](/methodology/).

## What types of facility exist in Albania?

Albanian health providers need a licence under Law 10 107/2009 (article 27), and the categories relevant here sit under decision VKM 538/2009, category II.6. They are:

| Licence | What it is | Fit for rhinoplasty? |
|---|---|---|
| B.1 | Hospital | Yes, if it has a surgical unit and anaesthesia |
| B.2 | Day hospital | Possible; ask where you stay overnight |
| A.4 | Medical centre with micro-surgery | Ask carefully what surgery and anaesthesia it covers |
| A.5 | Aesthetic centre offering medical service | Not suited to general anaesthesia |

Under these categories, a rhinoplasty under general anaesthesia should sit in a B.1 or B.2 licence. The categories are decided by the Ministry of Health. We have not seen every licence, and the register is not public in the form we would like, so confirm each case. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/) for the surgeon side.

## What do providers say about their own setting?

Providers describe themselves differently, and these are their own statements, not our findings. Hygeia describes itself as a private hospital, part of a Greek group, with a plastic surgery unit on the Tirana–Durrës highway. American Hospital presents itself as a private hospital group with several sites, and says its price includes surgeon, anaesthesia, stay, medication and follow-up. KEIT describes itself as a day hospital that serves Italian patients.

Naming a provider here means only that it describes its setting publicly. It is not an endorsement. All three claims should be matched with the licence in the QKB register. Accreditation claims are self-reported, and we found no public list of accredited institutions.

## Why does the setting matter?

The setting matters because of what happens if something goes wrong. The NHS says nose reshaping is usually done under general anaesthetic, takes about 1.5 to 3 hours and usually involves a stay of one or two nights. General anaesthesia calls for an anaesthetist, monitoring and the means to treat an emergency.

The NHS also lists rare risks including heavy nosebleeds, and general operative risks such as excessive bleeding, blood clots, infection and allergic reaction to anaesthesia, which it describes as small. A facility that can deal with them is the point of choosing a hospital.

## What should you ask about the facility?

Ask these questions and get answers in writing:

1. **Licence.** Legal name, NIPT and licence category. Is it active?
2. **Anaesthesia.** Is there a named anaesthetist for your operation? Is the anaesthetist a physician, and are they present for the whole operation and recovery?
3. **Beds.** Where do you stay the night? On site, in a hotel with a nurse, or at your own hotel?
4. **Emergency.** What happens if you need intensive care or a blood transfusion? Is there an agreement with a hospital nearby, and how far is it?
5. **Staff.** Who is on duty overnight? Is a doctor on site or only a nurse?
6. **Surgeon.** Does the surgeon operate here regularly, or visit for set days? Who covers when they leave?
7. **Records.** Will you receive an operative report, anaesthesia record and discharge letter in a language you can use?

A good clinic answers these without hesitation. If the answers are vague, that is information.

## How do you check the licence?

Check the licence in the QKB entity search by typing the clinic's legal name or NIPT, then look for an active II.6 licence. A non-official mirror (qkb.atlas-studio.eu) shows licence status per business, but treat it as a pointer, not as proof. The search needs a browser.

Match what the licence says to what the clinic promises. A place licensed as an aesthetic centre that offers surgery under general anaesthesia is a question for the clinic and, if unresolved, a reason to leave. See [red flags and unlicensed clinics](/safety/red-flags-unlicensed-clinics/).

## Is a hospital always better than a day hospital?

A hospital is not always better, but it normally offers more of what you may need if there are complications. A day hospital designed for same-day surgery can be a sound choice if overnight stay, an anaesthetist and an emergency transfer are arranged and written down. The question is not the label but the arrangement. If you are told you will go to a hotel after surgery, ask who looks in on you that night.

## What about the accreditation badges?

Badges on a website tell you what the clinic says about itself. No public list of accredited institutions was found in Albania, so a badge such as an international certification cannot be confirmed from a public list. Ask for the certificate with the issuer's name and date, then check with the issuer.
