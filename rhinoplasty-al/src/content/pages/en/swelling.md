Swelling is the part of rhinoplasty recovery that lasts longest and worries patients most. The nose swells quickly, shrinks in stages, and keeps changing for months. This guide sets out the stages the sources describe, so you can tell normal from not normal. Your surgeon's advice applies first.

## How long does the swelling last?

Most swelling settles in two to three months, and small amounts can last up to a year. MedlinePlus says most swelling improves by two to three months and that final results may not be visible for up to a year or longer. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) says swelling may take up to a year to completely subside and the final shape to emerge. Cleveland Clinic says full results usually appear after one year.

## Why does the nose hold swelling so long?

The soft tissue of the nose holds swelling longer than most parts of the body. ASPS says this in its recovery blog. Cleveland Clinic describes the swelling as occurring in and under the skin, making the nose look bigger than usual, and says bruising may appear around the nose and eyes.

## Month by month

| When | What sources describe |
|---|---|
| Days 1 to 7 | Pain and swelling are most pronounced (ASPS). Swelling, pain and headaches are common (MedlinePlus). Pain should lessen within days to a week (Cleveland Clinic). |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Bruising and tenderness on cheeks and under the eyes usually last a week or two (ASPS). The splint comes off at about one week or within two (ASPS, Cleveland Clinic). |
| Weeks 3 to 4 | "A marked difference" is visible and swelling continues to go down (ASPS). |
| Weeks 4 to 6 | Swelling, bruising and skin colour changes are still common (Cleveland Clinic). Initial results are visible at about one month (ASPS). |
| Months 2 to 3 | Most swelling improves (MedlinePlus). About 90% is gone after three months (Cleveland Clinic). |
| Months 3 to 12 | The tip may stay swollen and numb for several months (MedlinePlus). Mild morning swelling can last up to a year (Cleveland Clinic). |
| About 1 year | Full results usually appear (Cleveland Clinic). The final shape emerges (ASPS). |

## What is normal along the way?

Several things that look worrying are described as normal in the sources.

- **One side bigger than the other.** Cleveland Clinic says asymmetrical swelling in the first few weeks is a normal healing response.
- **Swelling that comes and goes.** ASPS says swelling may come and go and is often worse in the morning during the first year.
- **Numbness and a puffy tip.** MedlinePlus says the nose tip may remain swollen and numb for several months.
- **Colour changes.** Cleveland Clinic lists skin colour changes as common up to four to six weeks.

The tip tends to be the last area to settle. StatPearls notes the skin over the tip is the thickest and most oily part of the nose. The sources do not give separate timelines for a [bulbous tip](/nose-concerns/bulbous-tip/) or [thick skin](/procedures/ethnic-and-thick-skin-rhinoplasty/), so ask your surgeon what to expect for yours.

## What can you do about swelling?

Follow your surgeon's instructions. Cleveland Clinic advises keeping your head elevated, especially at night, using cool compresses (not ice) on the cheeks or under the eyes, avoiding facial impact and avoiding pressure on the bridge from glasses. StatPearls lists head-of-bed elevation, gentle icing of the midface and avoiding activities that raise blood pressure as nursing advice. Where those two differ on ice, follow the surgeon who treated you. See [aftercare once you are home](/recovery/aftercare-at-home/).

Do not start new treatments, taping or massage on your own unless your surgeon tells you to. We found no source we opened that supports doing so.

## What is not normal?

Call your surgeon if you have severe or worsening pain, a fever, yellow discharge, uncontrolled nosebleeds, or difficulty breathing. Cleveland Clinic lists difficulty breathing, nausea or vomiting, signs of infection such as fever or yellow discharge, severe pain and uncontrolled nosebleeds as reasons to call your provider. StatPearls says a septal haematoma should be recognised and drained promptly to prevent infection. A septal haematoma is a collection of blood in the septum. If you cannot reach your surgeon, read [problems after you get home](/safety/problems-after-you-get-home/).

## When can you judge the result?

Judge the result at about a year. StatPearls says surgical revision is generally delayed 6 to 12 months so swelling can settle. That timing is for the surgeon's judgement, not for a panicked decision in month two. If you are unhappy at one year, read [revision rhinoplasty](/procedures/revision-rhinoplasty/) and risks and complications.

Photograph the nose at the same angle and in the same light every few weeks. Photos help you and your surgeon see progress that a mirror hides.

## How do you keep track of progress?

Take a photo at the same angle and in the same light every week for the first month and then every month. Do not compare yourself with other patients, because noses, skin and operations differ. Look for the trend over weeks, not the difference from day to day, since ASPS says swelling can come and go and be worse in the morning. If you are worried that the swelling is not changing at all after several months, send your photos to the surgeon or to a local nose specialist. They can tell you whether it is within the range the sources describe and whether anything should be done.
