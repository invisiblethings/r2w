A rhinoplasty quote in Albania should list, in writing, every item you are paying for. A single number such as "€3,000" tells you very little. The same figure can cover a full hospital stay at one provider and only the surgeon's fee at another.

This guide gives you a checklist to hold against any quote. For the price ranges clinics publish, see [rhinoplasty prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/). For what tends to come on top, see [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).

## What should the core of the quote cover?

The core of a quote is the operation itself: the surgeon, the anaesthesia and the place where you are operated on. Check these first.

- **The operation.** Name the technique or at least the type: primary, septorhinoplasty, tip, revision. Septorhinoplasty also corrects the septum and is priced higher in published lists. Hygeia, for example, shows primary rhinoplasty at €2,000–3,800 and septorhinoplasty at €3,200–4,200 on its own site (undated).
- **The surgeon.** The quote should name who will operate. Some Tirana clinics work with visiting surgeons, so "our team" is not an answer. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).
- **Anaesthesia.** General anaesthesia is usual for rhinoplasty; the NHS says surgery is usually carried out under general anaesthetic. The quote should say it includes the anaesthetist and the drugs.
- **The facility.** Hospital, day hospital or clinic, and the nights of stay included. The NHS describes a typical stay of one or two nights for nose reshaping. See [hospital or clinic](/clinics/hospital-or-clinic/).

## What do Albanian providers say their prices include?

Two Tirana hospitals publish what is in their price, and the scopes differ. Hygeia says its range includes the surgeon, anaesthesia, the facility and follow-up during your stay, with hotel and flights extra. American Hospital says its €2,500–3,500 includes surgeon, anaesthesia, stay, medication and follow-up (its own FAQ, undated).

Neither page is a contract. Treat them as the starting point for questions, not as a guarantee of what your quote will say.

## What small items are easy to miss?

Small items are where quotes differ most. Ask whether each of these is in the price or billed separately:

| Item | Ask |
|---|---|
| Pre-operative blood tests and heart check | Included, or paid at the lab? |
| Splint, dressings, tapes | Included? How many changes? |
| Medication (antibiotics, painkillers) | For the stay only, or to take home? |
| Overnight room | One night or more? Single or shared? |
| Imaging or 3D simulation | Included in the consultation? |
| Consultation fee | Refunded if you book? |
| Interpreter or coordinator | Free, or charged per day? |
| Septum or alar base work | Part of the plan or an add-on? |

Turkish guides list a similar set. Acibadem International says Turkish prices usually cover consultation and planning, the operation with a consultant anaesthesiologist, one night's stay where planned, splint, dressings, medication and first follow-up checks. It adds that hotel and transfers appear in most international packages and should be confirmed in writing. Use that list as a model for what a complete quote looks like.

## What follow-up and revision terms should be in writing?

Follow-up and revision terms should be written down, because they decide what happens if the result is not what you expected. The NHS advises asking before booking what happens if complications arise, where follow-up appointments will be, and how easily you could return for further surgery.

Ask these questions:

1. How many check-ups are included while you are in Albania, and on which days?
2. Is there a review by video or photo after you return? Who answers, and in which language?
3. If a revision is needed, is it free, discounted or charged? Within what period?
4. If you need treatment at home, who pays? The clinic may not cover it.

Many clinics do not publish revision terms. Absence from a website is not a refusal; it means you must ask. A clinic that will not put the terms in writing has answered a different question.

## What do agency packages add or hide?

An agency "all-inclusive" package can bundle flights, hotel and transfers, but it adds a middleman. In the research behind this site, one agency listing from August 2022 advertised €2,990 "all inclusive"; its current offer is unverified. When a package is sold by an agency, ask which clinic and which surgeon you are actually booking, and what the agency is paid.

The NHS warns to be wary of surgery sold as part of a holiday, to meet the surgeon yourself rather than only sales staff, and not to pay for a hospital or surgeon you have not seen or met. That is useful advice for any package.

## How should you compare two quotes?

Compare quotes by putting both into the same table, line by line, and filling the gaps with questions. Do not compare totals until the scopes match.

1. Write the items above down the left side.
2. Mark each as included, extra (with price), or unknown.
3. Add the unknowns at a realistic cost, including [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/) such as flights, hotel and a second trip.
4. Compare the adjusted totals.

Lower price with more unknowns is often not cheaper. Also consider the cost of a lost safety margin: a quote that skips an overnight stay or an anaesthetist on site may be low for a reason. Our [questions to ask a clinic](/clinics/questions-to-ask/) guide turns this into a list you can send by email.

## What should you do before paying a deposit?

Before you pay a deposit, have the quote, the surgeon's name and the refund terms in writing. A deposit is usually a pledge to a date, so read what happens to it if you cancel or the surgeon cannot operate.

Check also that the amount is stated in euros or leks with the exchange basis, and that the payment goes to an account in the clinic's legal name, not a personal account. Never rely on a chat message alone.
