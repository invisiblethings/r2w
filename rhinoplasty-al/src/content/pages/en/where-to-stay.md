Where you stay matters less for comfort than for how easily you can reach the clinic and rest. For most of the stay you will be in your room with a splint on your nose, so choose a place that is quiet, easy to enter and close to help.

This guide gives criteria, not recommendations of particular hotels.

## What should you look for?

Look for access, quiet, distance and help close by. A short list:

- **Distance to the clinic.** Aim for a short ride, so a late-night problem or an early check-up is not a long trip.
- **Access.** A lift or a ground-floor room. Avoid steep stairs and cobbled approaches.
- **Quiet and darkness.** You will rest a lot.
- **A private bathroom** with a shower and enough space to move.
- **Food and a pharmacy nearby**, or a kitchen or room service.
- **Reception staff** who speak a language you share and can call a taxi or the clinic.
- **Flexible cancellation.** Your dates may change if the surgeon asks you to stay longer.

## What does the first night look like?

The first night is usually in the facility, depending on the clinic. The NHS says nose reshaping usually involves a hospital stay of one or two nights. Ask the clinic whether the first night is included in the quote and where you would sleep, and book your hotel from the day after. If the clinic plans for you to sleep at a hotel on the night of surgery, ask who will check on you and how you reach someone in an emergency. See [hospital or clinic](/clinics/hospital-or-clinic/).

## Where is the clinic?

The clinic's address should drive your choice. Tirana's providers are spread across the city; the research behind this site found all 26 listed entities in Tirana, with at least two hospitals on the Tirana–Durrës road in Kashar, outside the centre. A hotel in the middle of town may be convenient for sightseeing but far from a clinic outside it.

Ask the clinic:

1. Which hotels or apartments do your patients use, and why?
2. Do you provide a transfer between the airport, hotel and clinic? Is it in the price?
3. How long does the transfer take from the places you recommend?
4. Is there a hotel with a direct arrangement for patients? If so, is the price the same as on public sites?

A clinic-linked hotel can be handy, but compare it with others and read the cancellation terms.

## Hotel or apartment?

Either works. A hotel gives daily service and a front desk, which helps if you travel alone. An apartment gives more space and a kitchen, which helps if you have a companion and a longer stay. If you rent a private apartment, check the lift, the neighbourhood at night and how you would reach the host in an emergency.

| | Hotel | Apartment |
|---|---|---|
| Help on hand | Reception, room service | Host by phone |
| Space and kitchen | Limited | Usually more |
| Cancellation | Often flexible | Varies by host |
| Cleaning | Daily | Often on request |

## How should you rest?

Rest as your surgeon tells you. The NHS says you "need to rest" and advises avoiding alcohol, sun exposure, sightseeing and swimming, and not planning a holiday straight after surgery. It also says the dressing and splint stay on for 7 days, that you may have difficulty breathing through the nose for about a week, and that you may need up to 2 weeks off work.

Practical points:

- Bring the clinic's written aftercare instructions and keep them where you can read them.
- Keep your medicines and the clinic's phone number within reach.
- Bring light clothes that open at the front, so you do not pull anything over your nose. This is practical advice, not medical advice; ask your surgeon.
- Do not book tours for the days after the operation.

See [rhinoplasty recovery day by day](/recovery/day-by-day/).

## What if you need help?

If you need help, call the clinic first. GOV.UK gives Albania's emergency numbers as 112 for police, 127 for an ambulance and 128 for the fire service. It also warns that medical and dental facilities, including emergency, are very poor, particularly outside Tirana. That is a reason to stay near your clinic and to make sure the clinic has an emergency plan before you travel.

Save in your phone: the clinic's 24-hour number, the surgeon's number, the hotel's address in Albanian script and the address of the nearest hospital. Ask your hotel to help you tell a taxi driver where to go.

## What about a companion?

A companion makes a hotel stay easier. They can talk to reception, collect food and medicines, and accompany you to check-ups. Book a room that suits two people, and tell the clinic that someone will be with you. See [planning your trip](/travel/planning-your-trip/).
