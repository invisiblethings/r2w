Many people who ask for a nose job are really asking about the tip: too round, too wide, too low or too pointed. Tip rhinoplasty is surgery focused on that area. It can also be part of a full rhinoplasty.

This guide explains what tip work involves, why it is one of the harder parts of the nose to get right, and what to ask.

## What is tip rhinoplasty?

Tip rhinoplasty is surgery that reshapes the nasal tip, usually by changing the cartilage that supports it. ASPS lists enlarged or hooked nasal tips and tip shape among the concerns rhinoplasty can address. The surgeon may refine, reposition or support the cartilages so the tip is narrower, more defined or rotated differently.

Some surgeons do it through the nostrils. Others use an open approach. A closed rhinoplasty series lists isolated columellar, rim or tip corrections through pocket surgery as suitable for a closed approach, while open surgery is generally preferred for complex grafting. See [open rhinoplasty](/procedures/open-rhinoplasty/) and closed rhinoplasty.

## Who suits a tip-only operation?

You are a candidate if the tip is your main concern and the bridge and breathing are fine. Cleveland Clinic lists the general requirements: you should have finished growing, be in good health, not smoke, have realistic goals and want surgery for your own reasons. If your bridge has a hump or your nose is crooked, a tip-only plan may leave the problem you actually notice. A surgeon should assess the whole nose.

## Why is the tip hard to get right?

Tip problems feature heavily in studies of revision surgery. In a prospective study of 104 patients seeking revision rhinoplasty, published in 2010, tip asymmetry was the most common aesthetic concern for both patients and surgeons, followed by a crooked middle third and an irregular upper third ([HealthDay summary](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/ear-nose-and-throat/tip-asymmetry-most-common-reason-for-rhinoplasty-643388.html)).

A 2021 study of 252 revisions after open rhinoplasty found the revision rate was 10.8%. The leading reasons were insufficient tip rotation (37.7%), a hanging columella (30.2%) and a supratip deformity (28.6%). Strut grafts raised the risk of inadequate rotation about 5.3-fold compared with the tongue-in-groove technique, and low tip projection before surgery roughly doubled that risk. The data come from one retrospective study, but it shows why you should ask which technique your surgeon uses and why ([Plast Reconstr Surg 2021](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/20149738-f949-4049-957e-6c424cd28a83/revision-rhinoplasty-after-open-rhinoplasty-lessons-from-252-cases-and-analysis-of-risk-factors)).

## How much does skin thickness matter?

Skin thickness changes how visible the result is. A systematic review of thin skin finds that it gives minimal camouflage, so irregularities in bone or cartilage show, and aggressive tip sculpting may cause irregularities or shine-through. The review included 15 studies of 679 patients, most of them low-level evidence with short follow-up ([Cureus 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11383643/)).

A Saudi study of 154 patients found satisfaction did not differ significantly between thin, intermediate and thick skin, though it was a small, single-centre survey with a 62% response rate ([Saudi Med J 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6344664)). Read [bulbous tip](/nose-concerns/bulbous-tip/) and [ethnic and thick-skin rhinoplasty](/procedures/ethnic-rhinoplasty/) for more.

## What is recovery like?

Plan for a long wait before judging the tip. Cleveland Clinic lists packing removed within 24 to 48 hours, a splint for one to two weeks, return to work or school after one to two weeks, swelling peaking at four to six weeks, about 90% gone at three months and final results at about a year. On its service page it gives 12 to 18 months. Plan your judgement of the tip for after that, not for the week you fly home. See swelling month by month.

## What are the risks?

The risks are those of any rhinoplasty, plus a risk that the tip result is not what you pictured. ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance and revision surgery. Cleveland Clinic adds nosebleeds, a changed sense of smell and pain, and says about 15% of patients need a revision for small changes. Read [risks and complications](/safety/risks-and-complications/).

## What does it cost in Albania?

One Tirana private hospital publishes €2,500 to €3,200 for tip rhinoplasty and €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty (own site, undated; hotel and flights extra) ([price page](https://hygeia.al/plastic-surgery/facial-surgery/rhinoplasty-albania/)). The ranges overlap, so the label on a quote matters less than what it covers. Ask for the plan in writing, including grafts, and check what a quote should include.

## What should you ask the surgeon?

Ask how they will rotate and support the tip, whether they plan grafts, and which technique they use. Ask for healed photos of noses with your skin type and tip shape. Ask what the realistic change is, given your skin. If you are told the result will be perfect, treat that as a warning sign.

## How do you tell a good tip plan from a poor one?

A good plan explains the problem, the technique and the limit. The revision studies suggest what to ask. If your tip is low or under-rotated, ask how the surgeon will avoid insufficient rotation, which was the leading reason for revision in the 252-case series, and whether they use tongue-in-groove sutures or strut grafts. If you are older, the same study found age over 40 raised the risk of a hanging columella about 6.8-fold in that group, so ask how your age affects the plan. These are findings from one study, not rules, but they show the questions a prepared surgeon can answer.
