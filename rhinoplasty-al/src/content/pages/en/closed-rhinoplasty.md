Closed rhinoplasty is the approach where every cut is made inside the nostrils. The skin of the nose stays attached, and nothing shows on the outside afterwards. It can be an excellent option for the right nose and a poor one for the wrong nose.

This guide covers what closed rhinoplasty is, which noses it fits, what the evidence says about its trade-offs, and what to ask in Albania.

## What is closed rhinoplasty?

Closed rhinoplasty is a nose operation done through incisions inside the nostrils. Cleveland Clinic describes it as the approach for smaller reshaping, while open rhinoplasty, with an extra cut across the columella, is used for major reshaping ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11011-rhinoplasty)).

The surgeon works through pockets under the skin rather than lifting the skin off the nose. That means less of the structure is seen directly, which is the main reason surgeons choose between the two approaches case by case.

## Who is closed rhinoplasty suited to?

It tends to suit standard noses where the changes are limited. A single-surgeon series of 238 cases lists the upper two-thirds of the nose in standard cases, subtle supratip surgery, isolated columellar, rim or tip corrections through pocket surgery, and revisions involving limited dorsal augmentation ([Indian J Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9535069/)). The same team used it for reduction, augmentation, straightening and functional work.

If you are considering preserving the bony hump rather than cutting it down, a closed approach may be used with preservation techniques in thin-skinned noses. See [preservation rhinoplasty](/procedures/preservation-rhinoplasty/).

## What are the advantages?

The main advantages are no external scar and, according to one surgeon's experience, less tissue disturbance. The 238-case paper lists less extensive skeletonisation, less postoperative swelling, nerve dysfunction and recovery time than open surgery, and no risk of a visible columellar scar. It reports a complication rate of 0.8%.

Be careful with that number. The study was retrospective, from one surgeon, scored by a panel of three assessors, and the authors state that no valid single-surgeon comparison of open and closed outcomes or reoperation rates exists.

## What are the disadvantages and limits?

The limit is exposure. The same paper lists reduced visualisation of cartilage and bone as a disadvantage, says grafting is harder, and notes that the literature favours open surgery for complex grafting. It adds that the closed approach is criticised and losing favour among surgeons.

A 2025 prospective study of 30 primary patients (15 open, 15 closed) at one Indian hospital found both groups improved. Closed patients had more persistent nasal obstruction (20% against 0%), persistent deviation (26.7% against 13.3%), synechiae, meaning scar bands inside the nose (13.3% against 0%), and intraoperative septal flap tears (13.3% against 0%). The authors favoured open surgery for complex deformities. The study is small, was done in one centre and followed patients for 7 to 12 months, and has internal inconsistencies, so it is a signal to ask questions rather than a verdict ([Cureus 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12318243)).

## When is closed rhinoplasty not suitable?

It is generally not the first choice for severe deformity, major tip reconstruction or heavy grafting. The 2025 study authors favour open surgery for complex deformities, and the single-surgeon paper says complex grafting is easier open. Our guide to [open rhinoplasty](/procedures/open-rhinoplasty/) explains that route.

Cleveland Clinic also lists conditions that apply to any rhinoplasty: you should have finished growing, be in good health, not smoke, have realistic goals and want the change for yourself.

## What is recovery like?

Recovery follows the usual rhinoplasty pattern. Cleveland Clinic lists packing removed within 24 to 48 hours, a splint for one to two weeks, a return to work or school after one to two weeks, swelling and bruising peaking at four to six weeks, about 90% of swelling gone at three months, and final results after about a year. On its service page it gives 12 to 18 months for the last swelling to settle.

It is usually an outpatient operation of one to three hours, under general anaesthesia or local anaesthesia with sedation. Plan to have a driver and someone with you the first night. See [recovery day by day](/recovery/day-by-day/).

## What are the risks?

Risks are the general rhinoplasty risks. ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, septal perforation, poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance and the possibility of revision surgery. Cleveland Clinic adds nosebleeds, a changed sense of smell and pain, and says about 15% of patients need a revision to make small changes. A scar-free approach does not mean a risk-free one.

## What does it cost in Albania?

Published prices rarely separate open from closed. One Tirana private hospital lists technique-based ranges on its own site (undated): €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty, €3,200 to €4,200 for septorhinoplasty and €3,800 to €5,500 for revision. See [prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/). Ask for a written quote that states the approach, any grafts, anaesthesia and follow-up.

## What should you ask the surgeon?

Ask why they recommend closed for your nose, what they would do if the plan changed during surgery, and how many closed cases like yours they do. Ask whether they would switch to an open approach if needed and whether that is included in the quote. For help with a list, see [questions to ask a clinic](/clinics/questions-to-ask/).
