An online consultation is the cheapest way to test a clinic before you commit to a flight. It lets you see how the surgeon listens, what they propose and how clearly the clinic answers. It cannot replace an examination, and it should not be where you decide.

This guide explains what to expect, how to prepare and what to watch for. It is about logistics; the surgeon decides what is right for your nose.

## What can an online consultation do?

An online consultation can introduce the surgeon, discuss what bothers you, and set out a provisional approach and price range. It can also test practicalities: language, response time and whether questions get written answers.

The NHS advises that you should have two consultations with the surgeon before any surgery, with a cooling-off period between them, normally two weeks in the UK. When the clinic is abroad, an online call can serve as the first and an in-person meeting before the operation as the second. The NHS also advises asking the surgeon to explain the procedure, its limits, alternatives and risks, and to show before and after pictures of their work.

## What can it not do?

It cannot examine the inside of your nose, your breathing or the thickness of your skin. A video call and photos do not show the septum or how the cartilage will behave. Anything a clinic says about technique or result before an examination is provisional.

Be cautious if a clinic gives a fixed price and a promise of result after a message and three photos. Ask what could change the plan once the surgeon examines you in person, and what happens to the price and to your deposit if it does.

## How do you prepare?

Prepare photos, a short history and a list of questions. A good preparation sheet:

1. **Photos.** Front, both sides, three-quarter views and one from below, in even light with a plain background and no filters. Ask the clinic for its photo instructions.
2. **What you want.** A few sentences about what bothers you, and photos of noses you like, as a conversation starter, not a target.
3. **Your history.** Past nose surgery or injury, breathing problems, allergies, medicines, and other health conditions.
4. **Breathing.** Whether one or both sides are blocked. If you have breathing problems as well as a cosmetic concern, say so; see [septorhinoplasty](/procedures/septorhinoplasty/).
5. **Dates.** When you could travel and how long you can stay.
6. **Questions.** Use [questions to ask a clinic](/clinics/questions-to-ask/).

## Who should you speak to?

Speak to the surgeon who will operate. The NHS advises meeting the surgeon yourself rather than only sales staff, and not paying for a hospital or surgeon you have not seen or met. A coordinator can book the call and answer logistics, but not decide what is medically right for you.

Ask at the start: "Are you the surgeon who will operate on me?" If not, ask for a call with that surgeon. Check the surgeon's registration with [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/) before the call, so you can ask informed questions.

## What should you ask during the call?

Ask the questions you could not settle in writing:

- What do you see in my nose, and what do you suggest? What would you not do?
- What are the risks in my case? The NHS lists rare complications including permanent breathing difficulty and changed sense of smell.
- Have you operated on noses like mine? Can I see before and after photos?
- What happens if I am unhappy with the result?
- Who looks after me after the operation, and how do I reach you from home?

Take notes and ask for a written summary afterwards. A clinic that sends the summary, with the surgeon's name, is easier to trust than one that does not.

## How do you spot a sales call?

A sales call focuses on dates, deposits and discounts, not on your nose. Warning signs include:

- A price "valid today only" or a countdown on slots
- Pressure to pay a deposit before you have spoken to the surgeon
- Reluctance to name the surgeon or to put the offer in writing
- Promises about the result
- A discount for booking on the call

You are entitled to time. The NHS says you should be offered a cooling-off period after any consultation, and that you can walk away if you are not completely comfortable. See [red flags](/safety/red-flags-unlicensed-clinics/).

## What about body image and expectations?

If you find your thoughts about your nose are taking over your life, or no result seems enough, say so to the surgeon or a doctor you trust. A good surgeon will ask about your expectations, and may suggest speaking to a doctor or waiting. If body dysmorphic disorder is a concern, it is better raised before surgery than after. This is not a criticism of you.

## What happens after the call?

After the call, compare the clinic with others using [how to choose a clinic](/clinics/how-to-choose/). If you choose one, ask for the itemised quote ([what it should include](/cost/whats-included/)) and plan your dates ([planning your trip](/travel/planning-your-trip/)). Keep the call summary; you will want to compare it with what is said in person.
