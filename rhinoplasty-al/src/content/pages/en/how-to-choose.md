Choosing a clinic in Albania is mostly a process of elimination: you check a few things in a fixed order and drop any clinic that fails one. The order matters, because licence and surgeon checks cost nothing and can save you a bad decision.

This is the method we use on this site, written for you to apply yourself. It does not recommend any clinic. For how we assess clinics, see the [methodology](/methodology/).

## What should you check first?

Check the licence first, because everything else depends on it. Albanian health providers need a licence under Law 10 107/2009. Search the clinic's legal name or NIPT in the QKB entity search and look for an active II.6 licence. A rhinoplasty under general anaesthesia should sit in a hospital (B.1) or day hospital (B.2) licence. See [hospital or clinic](/clinics/hospital-or-clinic/).

No public online register of licensed clinics or doctors was found, so some checks need an email or a call. Treat that as normal, not as a reason to skip the checks.

## Who is the surgeon, and can you verify them?

The surgeon is the most important choice, and you should know their name before you pay anything. Many surgeons working in Tirana are Turkish or Italian and may visit on set days. Ask:

- Who will operate on me, by name?
- What is their specialty (plastic surgery or ENT) and where did they train?
- How many rhinoplasties do they do, and how many revisions?
- Are they registered in Albania, and in their home country if they are visiting?

The NHS suggests asking about training, professional memberships, years in practice and how many similar procedures a surgeon has done. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/), [Italian surgeons in Tirana](/clinics/italian-surgeons-in-tirana/) and [plastic surgeon or ENT](/surgeons/plastic-surgeon-or-ent/).

## What does the facility need?

The facility needs an anaesthetist, monitoring, overnight care and a plan for emergencies. The NHS says rhinoplasty is usually done under general anaesthetic and involves a stay of one or two nights. Ask where you sleep that night, who watches you, and where you would go if you needed intensive care.

## How do you compare quotes?

Compare quotes after you have confirmed the first three steps, and compare them line by line. Send each clinic the same description of what you want, and ask for an itemised written quote that names the surgeon, anaesthesia, stay, medication and follow-up. Use [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/) and the published ranges in [prices in 2026](/cost/prices-2026/) as a reference.

A price far below the others is a reason to ask what is left out. A price far above them is a reason to ask what you are getting. Either way, add the extras listed in [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).

## What evidence should you ask for?

Ask for evidence you can check, not for praise. Useful evidence:

| Evidence | What it tells you | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Licence number and category | The legal basis to operate | Verify in the QKB register |
| Surgeon's registration and diploma | Training and standing | Ask for the Order of Physicians confirmation |
| Before and after photos | Typical outcomes | The NHS says the surgeon should be able to show them; ask for consistent lighting and your nose type |
| Reviews | Patient experience | Scores and counts may be self-reported; read the critical ones and check how the clinic replied |
| Written aftercare and revision terms | What happens if the result disappoints | Many clinics do not publish them; ask |

## What are the warning signs?

Warning signs are pressure, vagueness and promises. Watch for urgency ("only a few places"), a price offered with no consultation, a surgeon who is not named, a refusal to give the licence number, requests to pay into a personal account, and guarantees about the result. The NHS advises being wary of surgery sold as part of a holiday and says you can walk away at any point if you are not comfortable. See [red flags and unlicensed clinics](/safety/red-flags-unlicensed-clinics/).

## What about aftercare once you are home?

Aftercare is what separates a good plan from a risky one. The NHS says overseas clinics may not provide follow-up treatment and advises asking before booking what happens if complications arise, where follow-up appointments will be and how easily you could return for further surgery. Ask the clinic:

1. How many check-ups are included in Albania, and when?
2. How do you reach the surgeon after you leave, and in which language?
3. What are the revision terms, in writing?
4. Will they send a report you can show a doctor at home?

Swelling can take up to 6 months to fully resolve, according to the NHS, so judge the result late and plan a review. See [aftercare at home](/recovery/aftercare-at-home/).

## How do you make the decision?

Make the decision when you can answer yes to each of these:

1. I have checked the licence.
2. I know the surgeon's name and have checked their registration.
3. The facility has an anaesthetist, overnight care and an emergency plan.
4. I have an itemised written quote and the terms.
5. I have spoken or video-called with the surgeon.
6. I have taken time to decide. The NHS says you should be offered a cooling-off period after any consultation.

If any answer is no, wait.
