A red flag is not proof that a provider is unsafe. It is a reason to stop, ask, and get the answer in writing before you pay. In Albania, where there is no public online register of doctors, red flags matter more because you cannot look the person up in one place. This guide lists the signs, explains what an unlicensed centre is, and shows how to check.

## What is an unlicensed clinic?

An unlicensed clinic is a business that provides health services without the licence Albanian law requires. Law 10 107/2009, article 27, makes licensing mandatory for anyone providing health services, and article 38 fines violations 500,000 lek, with higher penalties on repeat offences, according to the State Health Inspectorate (ISHSH).

Licences come in categories under VKM 538/2009. Category II.6 includes a medical centre with micro-surgery (A.4), an aesthetic centre offering a medical service (A.5), a hospital (B.1) and a day hospital (B.2). A beauty or "aesthetic" business with no medical licence is not allowed to operate on you. And in our reading, a rhinoplasty under general anaesthesia belongs in a B.1 or B.2 facility.

These are real cases, not theory. In October 2023 ISHSH found a dental clinic in Tirana had no licence for the service it was providing and fined it 500,000 lek, according to its own letter. That clinic was not a cosmetic provider. ISHSH's chief inspector said in March 2026, as reported by Shqiptarja, that licensing of clinics and offices doing invasive diagnostics and surgery would be disciplined in 2026, with plastic surgery in focus.

## Which signs about the clinic should worry you?

These signs concern the business.

- **No legal name, tax number (NIPT) or licence category.** A real clinic can give you these in one message. Without them you cannot search the business register.
- **No match in the QKB search.** Search the legal name or NIPT in the QKB entity search for an active category II.6 licence. A trading name, such as one on Instagram, may differ from the legal name; ask.
- **A licence that does not fit the operation.** Aesthetic centre (A.5) for an operation under general anaesthesia, with no hospital behind it, is a question to ask.
- **No anaesthetist named, or no overnight care or emergency transfer plan.** ASPS says safe surgery requires proper anaesthesia, sterile technique, modern equipment and trained surgeons.
- **Rooms or addresses that do not match.** The licence is for premises. Ask for the address where the operation takes place.
- **Only reviews as evidence.** Reviews and photos are marketing. Use them as a starting point only, never as proof of a licence.

## Which signs about the surgeon should worry you?

These signs concern the person who will operate.

- **The surgeon is not named** before you pay. Some clinics advertise a large team without saying who operates on noses.
- **The person fronting the clinic is not the operating surgeon.** Ask who will hold the scalpel and who will give the anaesthesia.
- **No Order of Physicians licence number or specialist diploma.** Law 123/2014 requires membership of the Order and an individual licence. There is no public online search, so ask for the number and ask the Order to confirm it in writing. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).
- **A visiting foreign surgeon with no authorisation to practise in Albania**, or no entry in their home register.
- **No before-and-after photos of their own patients,** or photos that look borrowed. The NHS says the surgeon should be able to show before-and-after pictures of their work.
- **You cannot speak to the surgeon.** The NHS says to avoid consultations that involve only sales staff, and that you should have two consultations with the surgeon before surgery.

## Which signs about the offer should worry you?

These signs concern the sale.

- **Pressure.** A deposit demanded within hours, a "today only" price or a "last slot" claim. Our [methodology](/methodology/) page explains how we check clinics instead.
- **A holiday package.** The NHS says to be cautious of holiday packages that sell surgery, and not to pay for a hospital you have never seen or a surgeon you have not met.
- **No itemised quote.** See [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/) and [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).
- **A price far below the others.** A very low price is not proof of a problem, but ask what has been left out.
- **No written follow-up plan,** or no answer to what happens if complications occur, where follow-up takes place, and how easily you could return. The NHS suggests these questions.
- **No revision policy in writing.**
- **Vague insurance.** The NHS says travel insurance normally does not cover problems arising from surgery.
- **Payment only to a personal account**, or in cash with no invoice.

## How do you check, step by step?

Do these in order and keep the replies.

1. Ask for the legal name, NIPT, licence category and the address of the operating room.
2. Search the name or NIPT in the [QKB entity search](https://qkb.gov.al/site/index.php/kerko-per-subjekt/). Look for an active II.6 licence.
3. Ask for the name, Order licence number and specialist diploma of every doctor who will treat you.
4. Ask the Order of Physicians to confirm them in writing. If you cannot get a confirmation, tell the clinic and decide whether you are comfortable.
5. Ask for an itemised quote and the aftercare and revision terms in writing.
6. Book two consultations, at least one with the operating surgeon, in a language you understand.

Our guides on [questions to ask a clinic](/clinics/questions-to-ask/) and [how to choose a clinic](/clinics/how-to-choose/) go further.

## What if you find a red flag?

Ask the clinic to explain in writing, and wait for the answer. If the explanation is vague, do not pay a deposit. You can walk away at any point before surgery. A good provider will not mind the questions. For the bigger picture, read is rhinoplasty in Albania safe?
