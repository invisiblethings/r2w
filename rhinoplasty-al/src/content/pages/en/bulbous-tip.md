A bulbous tip is a nasal tip that looks round, broad or poorly defined, often seen from the front. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) lists a nasal tip that is "enlarged, bulbous, drooping, upturned, or hooked" among the things rhinoplasty can address. Cleveland Clinic gives the same list.

Tip surgery is the most delicate part of rhinoplasty, and it is also the part where skin thickness matters most. This guide explains what a surgeon looks at, which techniques are used, and what result is realistic.

## What makes a tip bulbous?

A tip looks bulbous because of the cartilage under the skin, the skin itself, or both. The tip is shaped by a pair of lower lateral cartilages. If they are wide, rounded or boxy, the tip looks broad. StatPearls describes cephalic trimming as a way to reduce "a bulbous supratip", the area just above the tip.

Skin also matters. StatPearls notes that the skin and soft tissue over the nose is thinnest over the bridge and thickest and most oily in the lower third, which is the tip. A paper on complications of septorhinoplasty links a persistently wide tip to failure to account for thick, inelastic soft tissue, and to failure to straighten convex lateral cartilages. If your skin is thick, the cartilage can be refined, but the skin may not shrink to show it. See the guide to [ethnic and thick-skin rhinoplasty](/procedures/ethnic-and-thick-skin-rhinoplasty/).

## How does a surgeon assess a bulbous tip?

A surgeon assesses the tip by examining it, photographing it from several angles, and judging how the skin and cartilage will behave. StatPearls lists the standard steps:

- photographs from the front, sides, three-quarter, below and above;
- the thickness of the skin and soft tissue over the tip;
- measurement of tip projection and the angle between nose and upper lip;
- an exam of the airway, including the valves and any collapse of the nostril wall when you breathe in.

Cleveland Clinic adds that the surgeon examines and measures your face, and that you should bring photos of results you like. Ask the surgeon to show on a photo of you what they think can and cannot change.

## Which technique usually treats a bulbous tip?

A bulbous tip is usually treated with [tip rhinoplasty](/procedures/tip-rhinoplasty/), or as part of a full rhinoplasty. StatPearls describes these methods:

| Technique | What it does |
|---|---|
| Cephalic trim | Removes a strip from the top edge of the lower lateral cartilages to reduce a bulbous supratip. StatPearls says to leave 7 to 8 mm of lateral cartilage to prevent alar retraction. |
| Cephalic turn-in | Folds the cartilage inward and sutures it. This narrows the supratip and straightens irregular curves. |
| Lateral crural spanning graft | Pushes the cartilages outward to widen a narrow supratip. It is listed to show that the surgeon can both narrow and widen, depending on the nose. |
| Alar batten graft | Supports a nostril wall that collapses when you breathe in. |

Many surgeons also use sutures to reshape and narrow the tip, and some add small cartilage grafts to support it or add definition. A study of septorhinoplasty complications describes straightening the lateral cartilages with sutures or lateral crural struts when the tip stays wide.

On approach: StatPearls says the open approach exposes the full cartilage skeleton and is preferred for tip work, but it leaves a small scar on the columella (the strip between the nostrils) and causes more swelling. The endonasal approach makes delicate tip work harder. See [open rhinoplasty](/procedures/open-rhinoplasty/) and [closed rhinoplasty](/procedures/closed-rhinoplasty/).

## What result is realistic?

A realistic result is a tip that is narrower and better defined than before, within what your skin allows. It is rarely a sharp, pointed tip on thick skin.

Time matters. MedlinePlus says healing is gradual, the tip may remain swollen and numb for several months, and final results may not be visible for up to a year or longer. Cleveland Clinic says swelling is down by about 90% after three months, with minimal swelling that can persist up to a year. See swelling month by month.

ASPS also notes that results may not be completely symmetric.

## What can go wrong?

Tip surgery has the general risks of rhinoplasty, plus some that are specific. StatPearls says alar retraction can follow aggressive cephalic trimming, specifically when a strip of lateral cartilage at least 7 mm wide is not left intact. It also warns that nasal obstruction may follow collapse of the internal valve, or grafts that press on the external valve.

Tip stitches can also cause problems. In a hospital series of revision septorhinoplasty, four patients developed infection around a permanent suture in the tip about a year after surgery. The suture was replaced with an absorbable one. This is a small, single-hospital report, not a rate. Read the risks and complications guide for the whole picture.

If the tip needs a second operation, see revision rhinoplasty. StatPearls says revision is generally delayed 6 to 12 months so swelling can settle.

## How to choose a surgeon for tip work

Choose a surgeon who shows you tip results on skin like yours. Ask how many tip operations they do each year, whether they use grafts, and how they protect the nostril walls. Our questions to ask a clinic guide has a checklist, and how to verify a surgeon explains how to check licences in Albania.
