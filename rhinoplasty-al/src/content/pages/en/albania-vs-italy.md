Italian press consistently reports rhinoplasty in Albania as cheaper than in Italy, often by half. The gap is real in the published numbers, but those numbers are estimates, and the price of the operation is only part of what you will pay or risk.

This guide gives the figures with their dates, then looks at what the comparison leaves out. These are reported prices, not our survey, which is in progress. See [prices in 2026](/cost/prices-2026/).

## What does Italian press report?

Two Italian articles give a side-by-side estimate. Neither explains its method.

| Source | Date | Italy | Albania |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money.it | 10 May 2025 | €3,000–10,000 | €1,800–3,500 (source not attributed) |
| Panorama | 14 January 2024 | €5,000–8,000 | About €2,500 |

The Italian range is wide because surgeon, technique, clinic and city all vary. A rhinoplasty in a private hospital in Milan is not priced like one in a small town. An Albanian range of €1,800–3,500 is lower than the two Tirana hospitals publish on their own sites, which are about €2,000–3,800 (one hospital, primary) and €2,500–3,500 (the other). Treat the press figure for Albania as the low end.

## How big is the saving once you add travel?

The saving is the price difference minus everything you add by travelling. With a gap of a few thousand euros the saving may still be meaningful, but the lower end of the gap can shrink fast.

Add to the Albanian price:

- Return flights for you and a companion. Fares and routes depend on your city and dates; check them before you decide.
- Hotel for the recovery days. See [where to stay](/travel/where-to-stay/).
- Tests and medication not in the quote. See [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).
- A reserve for a second trip or a private consultation at home.

Then compare with an itemised quote from a surgeon in Italy, which may include a longer consultation, follow-up visits and a revision policy.

## What changes when the surgeon is far from home?

When the surgeon is far from home, follow-up becomes the main problem. The NHS advises asking before booking what happens if complications arise, where follow-up appointments will be and how easily you could return for further surgery, and warns that overseas clinics may not provide follow-up treatment. That advice applies equally to patients from Italy.

Practical points:

1. **Follow-up in Albania.** You will usually need a check-up about a week after surgery, when the splint comes off. The NHS says the dressing and splint stay on for 7 days. See [how long to stay in Albania](/recovery/how-long-to-stay-in-albania/).
2. **Follow-up in Italy.** Ask a surgeon at home in advance whether they would see you after surgery elsewhere. Some will, some will not. Keep your operative report, anaesthesia record, photographs and medication list.
3. **Revision.** If a revision is needed, who does it and who pays? Revision is rarely judged early, because the NHS says swelling can take up to 6 months to fully resolve and that the final result may take several months to appear. See [revision rhinoplasty](/procedures/revision-rhinoplasty/).
4. **Language.** Check that the surgeon, not only the coordinator, speaks a language you share. The NHS advises making sure the surgeon speaks a language you understand.

Panorama (January 2024) quotes Italian surgeons who say they see patients returning with complications from operations abroad; that article is about abroad in general and not specific to Albania.

## What about Italian surgeons working in Albania?

Some Tirana providers advertise Italian surgeons, and some of those surgeons may be registered in Italy and visit Tirana. That can reduce the language and follow-up gap, but it does not remove the need to check. A surgeon's Italian registration and their authorisation to operate in Albania are two different things. See [Italian surgeons in Tirana](/clinics/italian-surgeons-in-tirana/) and [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).

## How do licences and oversight differ?

In Italy, the home register to check is the FNOMCeO Albo. In Albania, the Order of Physicians keeps the register but we found no public online search; clinic licences can be checked through the QKB business register. Verification in Albania therefore takes written requests and more effort. We explain the steps in [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).

## What is the emergency number?

If you have a serious problem in Albania, the emergency numbers are 127 for an ambulance and 112 in EU countries. In Italy the number is 112. Keep your surgeon's direct number and the clinic's address on your phone.

## How should you decide?

Decide by comparing two complete, itemised quotes: one from an Albanian provider and one from a surgeon in Italy.

- Same operation described in words by both.
- Same level of facility: hospital or day hospital with an anaesthetist.
- Total including travel, hotel and a reserve.
- Follow-up and revision terms in writing.

If the Italian quote is within reach and follow-up at home matters most to you, the saving may not justify the distance. If the Albanian surgeon is verified, the setting is a licensed hospital and the follow-up plan is clear, the saving may be worth it. The decision is yours, and you can still walk away at any point; the NHS says so explicitly.
