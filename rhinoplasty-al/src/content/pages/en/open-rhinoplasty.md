Open rhinoplasty is the approach where the surgeon makes one extra cut on the outside of the nose, then lifts the skin to work on the structure underneath. It is common for tip surgery and for noses that need grafts. It is not automatically the better choice, and it is not needed for every nose.

This guide explains what the technique involves, who it suits, what you give up, and what to ask before you book in Albania.

## What is open rhinoplasty?

Open rhinoplasty is a nose operation that uses incisions that separate the nose's skin from the bone and cartilage so the surgeon can reshape them. Cleveland Clinic describes it as the approach for major reshaping, in contrast with closed rhinoplasty, where all incisions are inside the nostrils ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11011-rhinoplasty)).

In the comparative study published in *Cureus* in 2025, the open group had a transcolumellar incision, meaning a cut across the columella, the strip of tissue between the nostrils. The authors say open surgery gives better exposure ([Cureus 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12318243)).

## Who is open rhinoplasty suited to?

Open rhinoplasty tends to suit noses with complex deformities, tip problems that need grafting, and many revisions. The 2025 study authors favoured it for complex deformities. A review of closed rhinoplasty, from surgeons who still use that approach, agrees that grafting is harder through closed incisions and that the literature favours open surgery for complex grafting ([closed rhinoplasty series](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9535069/)).

It may be more than you need if the change is small. The same closed-rhinoplasty paper lists subtle supratip surgery, isolated tip corrections and limited dorsal work as cases where a closed approach can work. Read more in our guide to [closed rhinoplasty](/procedures/closed-rhinoplasty/).

## How is the operation done?

The surgeon makes the columellar incision, lifts the skin and soft tissue off the framework, reshapes or supports the cartilage and bone, then lays the skin back and closes the incision. Cleveland Clinic says rhinoplasty is done under general anaesthesia, or local anaesthesia with IV sedation, usually as an outpatient, and takes one to three hours. You go home the same day with a driver and a companion for the first night.

If you also have breathing problems, the septum can be straightened at the same time. See [septorhinoplasty](/procedures/septorhinoplasty/).

## What does it cost you: scar, time and numbness?

The main costs are a small external scar and a longer, more extensive operation. In the 2025 study, one of 15 open patients had visible columellar scarring, and the authors say open surgery needed more dissection and a longer operative time. They did not measure the time.

A review of closed surgery notes that the closed approach avoids a visible columellar scar and reports less swelling and less nerve dysfunction than open surgery. That paper is a retrospective, single-surgeon series, so treat the comparison as an opinion from one team, not proof.

## How does recovery compare?

Recovery follows the general rhinoplasty pattern. Cleveland Clinic lists packing removal within 24 to 48 hours, a splint for one to two weeks, a return to work or school after one to two weeks, swelling and bruising that peak at four to six weeks, and about 90% of swelling gone at three months. Final results usually appear after about a year.

On one Cleveland Clinic service page, the figure for full resolution of swelling is 12 to 18 months. Your own timeline will depend on your skin and what was done. For a day-by-day view, see recovery day by day.

## What are the risks?

Risks are the same as for any rhinoplasty. ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, septal perforation (rare), poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance, and the possibility of revision surgery. Cleveland Clinic adds nosebleeds, a changed sense of smell and pain.

In the 2025 comparison, open patients had more nosebleeds (13.3% against 6.7%) and one had an alar cartilage injury. The closed group had more persistent obstruction and deviation. With only 30 patients, one centre and 7 to 12 months of follow-up, these numbers show a direction, not a rate you can plan around. Our [risks and complications](/safety/risks-and-complications/) guide goes through the full list.

## What does open rhinoplasty cost in Albania?

Prices depend on the technique, the surgeon and what the quote includes. One Tirana private hospital publishes technique-specific ranges on its own website (undated): €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty, with septorhinoplasty at €3,200 to €4,200 and revision at €3,800 to €5,500. Hotel and flights are extra. Other sources on record put primary rhinoplasty at about €1,800 to €4,500. See [prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/) for dated sources and what a quote should include.

A price labelled "rhinoplasty" may not say whether it assumes an open or closed approach, or grafts. Ask.

## How do you choose a surgeon for an open rhinoplasty?

Choose by the surgeon's training and by how many noses like yours they have operated on, not by price or by the approach name. Ask which approach they would use on your nose and why, how they handle the columellar scar, and whether you can see healed photos of similar cases. ASPS stresses that you should raise all your questions directly with your surgeon.

In Albania there is no public register of surgeons, so verification takes extra steps. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).

## What if you are unsure whether you are a good candidate?

You are a better candidate if you have finished growing, are in good health, do not smoke, understand the limits of surgery and want it for your own reasons, according to Cleveland Clinic. If your main hope is to change how others see you, or you worry about a flaw others do not notice, say so at the consultation. A good surgeon will take that seriously and may suggest waiting.
