Piezo rhinoplasty is a way of cutting the nasal bones. It is sold to patients as a gentler method that means less bruising. There is research behind that claim, though it is not as strong as marketing suggests.

This guide explains what the technique is, what randomised trials show, and the drawbacks.

## What is ultrasonic (piezo) rhinoplasty?

Piezo rhinoplasty is a nose operation in which an ultrasonic instrument cuts the bones, instead of a manual chisel or saw. A 2022 systematic review describes piezosurgery as a technique in which piezoelectric crystals generate vibrations that cut bone along the planned line. It was first used in rhinoplasty in 2004 ([Aesthetic Plast Surg 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10229687/)).

Piezo changes how the bones are cut (the osteotomies). The rest of the operation, including the incision, the tip work and the septum, is the same as in other rhinoplasty. It only applies to the bone-cutting step, so if your plan does not involve cutting bone, there is nothing for piezo to do.

## What do the studies show about bruising and swelling?

Reviews of randomised trials suggest less early bruising and swelling. A 2024 meta-analysis of 12 randomised trials (292 piezo and 338 conventional cases) found less swelling on days 2 and 7 and less bruising on days 2, 4 and 7. The benefit looked larger when an external (open) approach was used, though that difference was not statistically significant ([J Clin Med 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11242129/)).

A 2022 review of eight trials with 440 patients found less short-term eyelid swelling in 75% of studies and less early bruising in 87.5%. Swelling reduction lasted through the whole follow-up in 50% of studies, and bruising reduction in 75%.

## What about pain, complications and operating time?

Piezo may reduce pain and some complications, without clear effects on operating time. The 2024 meta-analysis found lower pain scores on days 1 and 2, less need for painkillers and fewer complications overall, mainly mucosal injuries. Postoperative bleeding did not differ significantly, and operating time did not differ significantly either.

The 2022 review is more cautious on time. One study found longer surgery with piezo and others found no significant difference. It also lists possible thermal burns to the surgeon and a risk of incomplete (greenstick) fractures as potential drawbacks.

## How strong is the evidence?

The evidence is limited. The 2024 authors state that the certainty is low because of high variation between studies and questionable methods. Swelling and bruising were scored subjectively, several outcomes rested on one or two trials, and follow-up was only about a week. They describe their findings as hypothesis-generating and call for long-term studies, patient-reported outcomes and cost analysis. The 2022 review likewise notes that follow-up of under seven days limits firm conclusions.

In plain terms, the studies suggest the first week may be more comfortable. They do not show that the nose heals into a better shape, or that you can skip the long wait for swelling to settle.

## What are the drawbacks?

The main drawbacks are cost, a learning curve, and uncertain long-term benefit. The 2022 review says the device is more expensive than conventional tools and that separate tips must be purchased. It says inexperience may lengthen the operation, though it expects this to improve with practice. Ask how many piezo rhinoplasties your surgeon has performed.

## When is piezo not needed?

You may not need it if your operation does not involve cutting bone, such as some tip-only surgery, or if you are comfortable with a conventional approach in the hands of an experienced surgeon. A skilled surgeon using a chisel may serve you better than a less experienced one using a newer device. See [tip rhinoplasty](/procedures/tip-rhinoplasty/) for surgery focused on the tip.

## What is recovery like?

Recovery follows the general rhinoplasty pattern, and piezo does not remove the need for patience. Cleveland Clinic lists a splint for one to two weeks, return to work or school after one to two weeks, swelling and bruising peaking at four to six weeks, about 90% of swelling gone at three months and final results at about a year. Even if early bruising is less, plan your trip and your judgement of the result around this timeline. See [recovery day by day](/recovery/day-by-day/).

One Tirana private hospital lists piezo as an extra of €400 to €800 on top of the base price (own site, undated). Its base ranges are €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty and €3,200 to €4,200 for septorhinoplasty ([price page](https://hygeia.al/plastic-surgery/facial-surgery/rhinoplasty-albania/)). Ask whether the quote includes piezo or adds it. Compare with [prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/).

## How should you weigh it?

Weigh a possible gain in early comfort against cost and uncertainty. The 2024 meta-analysis suggests lower early swelling, bruising and pain, and the authors say the findings are hypothesis-generating. The 2022 review adds that the benefit lasted through follow-up in only some studies and that follow-up was short. If the extra cost is modest and your surgeon is experienced with the device, many patients may reasonably choose it. If the extra cost is large, or it is the main reason you picked a clinic, remember that a surgeon's skill with the whole operation matters more than one instrument.

Also ask whether the surgeon will use it on every case or only when the bones need to be cut in particular ways, and what they use if the device is unavailable on the day.
