A good rhinoplasty trip is planned backwards from the day you can safely fly home. The operation is one day; the checks before it and the recovery after it are what take time. This guide gives a timeline and a checklist.

It covers travel and logistics, not medical advice. Your surgeon decides when you can fly and what you can do.

## What is the order of steps?

The order of steps is: choose and verify, consult, quote, decide, book travel, travel, recover, review. Do not book flights before the surgeon and the quote are settled.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Weeks before | Shortlist clinics with [how to choose a clinic](/clinics/how-to-choose/); send [questions](/clinics/questions-to-ask/); verify the surgeon ([how](/surgeons/how-to-verify/)) |
| Weeks before | Online consultation with the surgeon; see [online consultation](/travel/online-consultation/) |
| Weeks before | Itemised written quote; time to decide; deposit only after the checks |
| 2–4 weeks | Book changeable flights, a place to stay, insurance; check your passport |
| Days before | Confirm the arrival plan with the clinic; pack; arrange work and family cover |
| In Albania | Consultation in person, operation, first check-up, splint removal, clearance to fly |
| After | Photo or video reviews; follow-up at home |

The NHS advises planning for two consultations before surgery, which gives a cooling-off period, usually two weeks in the UK. When the clinic is abroad, one can be online. Use the time.

## How long should you stay?

Stay at least until the first check-up and splint removal and until your surgeon clears you to fly. The NHS says the dressing and splint stay on for 7 days and stitches are usually removed around one week in. It also advises avoiding flying for 7 to 10 days after facial procedures because air travel after surgery raises the risk of blood clots.

So a stay of about a week is a common minimum; your surgeon may advise longer or shorter. Book a changeable return ticket. See [how long to stay in Albania](/recovery/how-long-to-stay-in-albania/) and [flying after rhinoplasty](/recovery/flying-after-rhinoplasty/).

The NHS also says you may need up to 2 weeks off work, so check your leave.

## What documents and insurance do you need?

You need a valid passport, travel insurance that fits the trip and the clinic's paperwork. GOV.UK says UK passport holders can enter Albania visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180 days, and the passport should be valid for at least 3 months after the day you plan to leave. That page covers British citizens only; other nationalities should check the rules with an Albanian embassy before they book.

On insurance, GOV.UK advises appropriate travel insurance for local treatment and medical evacuation, and having accessible funds to pay for care. The NHS says standard travel insurance usually will not cover problems arising from surgery, so read the exclusions and ask the clinic what its own insurance covers. See [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).

Bring:

- Passport, insurance documents and the clinic's written quote
- Your medical history and a list of medicines and allergies, in English or a language the clinic uses
- Photographs of your nose from before, for the surgeon and for your own records
- Contact details for the surgeon and the clinic

## What about a companion?

A companion is worth planning for. After anaesthesia you will be tired, your nose will be blocked and you will have a splint. Someone who can help with transfers, food and questions makes the first days easier. The sources we used do not cover companions, so this is practical advice, and you should ask the clinic whether it provides anyone to accompany you or interpret.

## Where should you stay?

Stay somewhere close to the clinic, quiet and easy to reach from the airport. The NHS advises resting after surgery and avoiding alcohol, sun, sightseeing and swimming, and not planning a holiday straight after the operation. See [where to stay](/travel/where-to-stay/).

## What should you know on arrival?

On arrival, confirm the transfer, the clinic's address and a 24-hour phone number. Note the emergency numbers: GOV.UK lists 112 for police, 127 for an ambulance and 128 for the fire service in Albania. GOV.UK also warns that medical and dental facilities, including accident and emergency, are very poor, particularly outside Tirana, so it matters that your clinic has its own emergency plan. Ask about it in advance; see [hospital or clinic](/clinics/hospital-or-clinic/).

Carry enough money or access to funds to cover unexpected costs, and keep a copy of your quote and receipts.

## What should you do before flying home?

Before flying home, get the surgeon's clearance to fly and a written summary. Ask for:

1. An operative report and a discharge letter.
2. A medication list and instructions.
3. The date and method of the next review (photos or video).
4. The surgeon's contact details and a plan if something worries you.

See [aftercare at home](/recovery/aftercare-at-home/) and [problems after you get home](/safety/problems-after-you-get-home/).
