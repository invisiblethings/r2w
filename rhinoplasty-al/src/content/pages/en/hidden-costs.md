The hidden costs of rhinoplasty abroad are the items that sit outside the surgeon's price: travel, accommodation, extras billed on the day, and the cost of problems after you return. None of them is a trick. They are simply not in a headline number, so you have to add them.

This guide lists them so you can build your own total. For what a good quote lists, see [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/). For published prices, see [prices in 2026](/cost/prices-2026/).

## What is usually not in the price?

Travel and accommodation are usually not in the price. Hygeia says on its own site that hotel and flights are extra, and that is common. Packages sold by agencies may include them, but then you should check what the clinic itself receives.

Items to add to your own budget:

- **Flights** for you and, if you bring one, a companion. Fares change by season; check them for your dates.
- **Hotel or apartment** for the whole stay, not just the night after surgery. See [where to stay](/travel/where-to-stay/).
- **Airport and clinic transfers** and local transport for check-ups.
- **Food, phone data, pharmacy items** such as saline spray, which may be small but add up.
- **Time off work.** The NHS says you may need up to 2 weeks off work after nose reshaping.

## Which extras can appear on the clinic bill?

Extras on the clinic bill are usually tests, medication and add-on procedures. A Bookimed article on Turkey notes that several patient reviews mention extra charges for medications or an echocardiogram, and it advises asking for an itemised quote. The same caution applies in Albania.

Ask whether these are in the quoted price:

- Blood tests, ECG or heart ultrasound before anaesthesia
- Medication taken at home
- An extra night if you are not fit to leave
- Septum correction or alar base reduction added during the operation. Hygeia lists alar base work separately at €800–1,200 and ultrasonic (piezo) technique as an add-on of €400–800 (own site, undated).
- Cartilage graft material, if the surgeon needs more than the septum provides

The point is not that these charges are unfair. It is that a surgeon may find, during the operation, that a plan needs adding to. Ask beforehand how that is priced.

## What does staying longer cost?

Staying longer costs hotel nights and sometimes a changed flight. You will usually be seen for a check-up and splint removal about a week after surgery; the NHS says the dressing and splint stay on for 7 days and stitches are usually removed around one week in.

The NHS also says to avoid flying for 7 to 10 days after facial procedures because air travel after surgery raises the risk of blood clots. Your surgeon may advise differently, and you should follow the surgeon's advice. If your return flight is fixed for day 3, you may pay to change it. See [when you can fly after rhinoplasty](/recovery/flying-after-rhinoplasty/) and [how long to stay in Albania](/recovery/how-long-to-stay-in-albania/).

Book flexible or changeable tickets. A cheap non-changeable fare can cost more than a flexible one if your plans move.

## What does insurance really cover?

Insurance may cover much less than you assume. The NHS says to check what the clinic's insurance covers, and that standard travel insurance usually will not cover problems arising from surgery.

Before you book:

1. Ask the clinic for its professional indemnity or liability insurance in writing, and what it covers if you are abroad.
2. Read your travel policy for exclusions on elective and cosmetic surgery.
3. Ask whether a specialist medical-travel policy exists in your country, and what it excludes.

## What if you need care after you get home?

If you need care after you get home, you may pay for it. The NHS warns that overseas clinics may not provide follow-up treatment, and recommends asking before booking what happens if complications arise and how easily you could return.

Costs to consider:

- A local consultation with a surgeon at home, which may be private and charged even for a brief check
- A second trip to Albania for revision, with flights and hotel again
- Revision surgery at home if you choose a different surgeon
- Time off work again

Public health systems differ. Some will treat emergencies but not elective follow-up or revision. For what to do if something goes wrong, see [problems after you get home](/safety/problems-after-you-get-home/).

## How do you budget for this?

Budget by adding every line to the quoted price and a margin for the unknown. A simple sheet works:

| Line | Your estimate |
|---|---|
| Quoted surgery price | |
| Flights (return, changeable) | |
| Hotel (nights = stay recommended + 2) | |
| Transfers and local transport | |
| Tests, medication not included | |
| Time off work | |
| Travel insurance covering surgery | |
| Reserve for a return trip or care at home | |

Add the lines, then compare with the quote from a clinic at home. The gap between countries may be smaller or larger than a headline suggests, so compare complete totals only.
