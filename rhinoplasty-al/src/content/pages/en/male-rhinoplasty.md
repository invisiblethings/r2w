Many men delay rhinoplasty for years, or only consider it after a broken nose or a long-standing breathing problem. Rhinoplasty for men is the same set of operations as for women. What differs is the planning, and what a natural result looks like.

This guide explains what surgeons weigh in male patients and what to ask, with a fair look at what is and is not supported by evidence.

## Is male rhinoplasty a different operation?

No, male rhinoplasty uses the same techniques, planned for different anatomy and goals. The Aesthetic Society, a professional body, says bone, skin and cartilage sizes differ between the sexes, that women's noses are generally shorter, and that the surgeon should account for these differences in the consultation, in surgery and in recovery ([Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/facial-procedures/articles/rhinoplasty-how-gender-plays-roll-your-nose-surgery)). The article is patient education, not a clinical study, and describes general tendencies.

## What do men usually ask for?

Men often ask for a distinct, straight and proportionate nose, and may raise a hump, a crooked nose, asymmetry or a nose that seems too large. The Aesthetic Society describes the male bridge as typically straight, and a slightly indented bridge as bringing out more feminine features. For the tip, it describes a tip that is proportional to the stronger bridge.

Your own face and wishes come first. Say what you want to keep: a strong profile, a family resemblance, a slight bump. A hump reduction that is too aggressive can leave a nose that looks operated on. See dorsal hump.

## How does thicker skin affect the plan?

Men's nasal skin is thicker than women's according to the Aesthetic Society, and skin thickness is one of the things a surgeon plans around. A systematic review of thin skin found that thin skin gives minimal camouflage for the underlying framework. Thicker skin is not covered by that review. A Saudi study of 154 patients found satisfaction did not differ significantly between thin, intermediate and thick skin groups, but it was a single-centre study with a 62% survey response ([Saudi Med J 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6344664)).

Practically, ask the surgeon what they can and cannot change on your skin and to show examples with similar skin. Our guide to [ethnic and thick-skin rhinoplasty](/procedures/ethnic-and-thick-skin-rhinoplasty/) goes into this.

## Is breathing part of the decision?

Often, yes. Cleveland Clinic describes functional rhinoplasty as restoring form and breathing after disease, cancer treatment, trauma or birth defects, and says septoplasty can be done alongside it. A broken nose from sport or an accident is a common route into the clinic. If your breathing is blocked, say so even when appearance is your main reason, so the surgeon examines the airway. See [septorhinoplasty](/procedures/septorhinoplasty/).

## What is recovery like?

Recovery is the same as for anyone and does not depend on sex. Cleveland Clinic lists packing removal within 24 to 48 hours, a splint for one to two weeks, work or school after one to two weeks, swelling and bruising that peak at four to six weeks, light exercise from about that time with heavy lifting avoided, about 90% of swelling gone at three months, and final results after about a year.

If your work is physical or you play contact sport, tell the surgeon at the consultation. Plan for the dates they give you rather than for the earliest dates anyone quotes. See recovery day by day.

## What are the risks?

The risks are those of any rhinoplasty. ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, septal perforation, poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance and the possibility of revision surgery. Cleveland Clinic adds nosebleeds, a changed sense of smell and pain. Read risks and complications.

## Is male rhinoplasty right for you?

You are a better candidate if you have finished growing, are healthy, do not smoke, have realistic goals and want the change for yourself, according to Cleveland Clinic. If you are worried about a flaw other people cannot see, or you feel a nose change would fix how you feel about yourself, it can help to say so to the surgeon or your doctor. Preoccupation with appearance can be a sign of body dysmorphic disorder, which is treatable. Asking about it is a normal part of a careful consultation.

## What does it cost in Albania?

Prices do not depend on sex. One Tirana private hospital publishes €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty, €3,200 to €4,200 for septorhinoplasty and €3,800 to €5,500 for revision (own site, undated; hotel and flights extra) ([price page](https://hygeia.al/plastic-surgery/facial-surgery/rhinoplasty-albania/)). Other sources on record range lower. See [prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/).

## How should you choose a surgeon?

Ask to see before and after photos of men, not only women, and ask how the surgeon keeps the nose proportionate to the face. Ask who will operate, since the clinic's consultant may not be the surgeon. Our guide on [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/) explains the checks that are possible in Albania.

## What if the nose was broken?

If the nose was injured, tell the surgeon when and how. Cleveland Clinic lists injury, including a broken nose or facial fractures, among causes of functional breathing problems. A healed fracture may have left a deviated septum or a crooked bridge that needs both functional and cosmetic work. Bring any scans or earlier medical records. Do not expect to have all of it corrected without a plan: some injuries leave scar tissue and changed structure that make the operation more complex, and your surgeon should say so.
