Wide or flared nostrils are one of the concerns people bring to a rhinoplasty consultation. Alar base reduction is the usual way to change them. It is a small operation with little room for error, because the nostril is a visible, delicate shape.

This guide covers what it does, what can go wrong, and how it is priced. The published evidence we could open is limited, and we say where.

## What is alar base reduction?

Alar base reduction is surgery that narrows the base of the nose by removing small pieces of tissue near the nostrils. ASPS lists nostril size and position among the concerns rhinoplasty can address ([ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/rhinoplasty)). A 2021 review explains that nasal sill excision, with or without alar wedge excision, reduces alar flare and changes nostril size, shape and angle, and is often part of alar base reduction ([Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8131461/)).

The alar is the wing of the nostril. The sill is the floor of the nostril, where it meets the lip. The surgeon decides which tissue to remove and how much, from your anatomy and goals.

## Who is it suited to?

It suits people whose nose is wide at the base or whose nostrils flare, and who accept small scars close to the nostril. In the expert-opinion paper on ethnicity and nonsurgical rhinoplasty, wide alae and a broad base are listed as typical features in some noses, such as Arab and Hispanic noses ([Aesthet Surg J Open Forum 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9337225)). These are generalisations and your nose may differ.

Not every wide-looking nose needs it. A wide tip or a wide bridge may be the real cause, and narrowing the nostrils will not fix those. A surgeon should look at the nose as a whole. See [wide nose](/nose-concerns/wide-nose/) and bulbous tip.

## How is it done?

The surgeon excises a small piece of the sill, a wedge from the alar wing, or both, then closes the incision with fine stitches. The 2021 review describes sill excision as a part of alar base reduction and alar wedge excision as the other option. We did not open a source that describes the step-by-step method, anaesthesia choice or operating time, so ask the surgeon what they plan for you.

It is described in the literature as a part of rhinoplasty, so it may be planned with the rest of the surgery. See open rhinoplasty.

## What are the risks?

The best-documented risk is removing too much. The 2021 review notes that improper resection of the nasal sill soft tissue can cause deformities, such as tear-shaped nostrils. It is a note in a paper mainly about reconstructing the sill, and it reports minimal complications in a 54-patient series using that repair, a small and non-comparative study.

Visible scars near the nostril are an inherent trade-off of the incisions. We did not open a source that gives the frequency of scarring, asymmetry or other problems, so we cannot quote numbers. General rhinoplasty risks also apply: ASPS lists anaesthesia risks, changes in skin sensation, difficulty breathing, infection, poor wound healing or scarring, skin discolouration and swelling, an unsatisfactory appearance and the possibility of revision surgery ([ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/rhinoplasty/safety)). Read [risks and complications](/safety/risks-and-complications/).

## What is recovery like?

For alar base reduction on its own, we did not find a source with a recovery timeline. As part of a rhinoplasty, Cleveland Clinic lists packing removed within 24 to 48 hours, a splint for one to two weeks, work or school after one to two weeks, swelling peaking at four to six weeks and about 90% of swelling gone at three months. Final results take about a year. Ask your surgeon when stitches come out and how to care for the incisions. See recovery day by day.

## Can alar base reduction be reversed?

Not easily. Removed tissue cannot simply be put back, which is why the 2021 review describes techniques to rebuild the nasal sill with cartilage grafts when too much has been taken. That is a technical repair with its own limits. The practical lesson is to be conservative and to ask for a plan that can be adjusted. See [revision rhinoplasty](/procedures/revision-rhinoplasty/).

## What does it cost in Albania?

One Tirana private hospital publishes €800 to €1,200 for alar base reduction, alongside €2,000 to €3,800 for primary rhinoplasty (own site, undated; hotel and flights extra) ([price page](https://hygeia.al/plastic-surgery/facial-surgery/rhinoplasty-albania/)). Other clinics may include it in a full rhinoplasty price. Compare quotes line by line using [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/) and [prices in Albania](/cost/prices-2026/).

## What should you ask the surgeon?

Ask whether alar base reduction is really what your nose needs, or whether the tip or bridge is the cause. Ask how much tissue they expect to remove, where the scar will sit, and how many they have done. Ask for healed photographs from the front, the side and from below. If they offer to remove a large amount to make a dramatic change, ask about the risk of distorting the nostril.

## Is it right for you?

It may be, if you have finished growing, are healthy and have realistic goals, as Cleveland Clinic lists for rhinoplasty in general, and if you understand that the change is small and the scar is permanent. A change of a few millimetres at the nostril can be visible on a face, so ask for a simulation or marked photographs and agree on the target before the day of surgery. Do not accept pressure to add the procedure at the last minute, and if you are unsure, leave it out. A small second correction is usually easier than repairing too much removal.
