On published prices alone, Albania and Turkey are closer than many people expect. The ranges overlap, the sources define their numbers differently, and the cost of travel and follow-up can outweigh a difference of a few hundred euros.

This guide sets out what Turkish sources publish, what is published for Albania, and which non-price questions decide the matter. These are published figures, not our survey. Our own Albanian price survey is in progress; see [prices in 2026](/cost/prices-2026/).

## What do Turkish sources publish?

Two sources we opened give Turkish ranges, and they differ in currency, date and scope.

| Source | Date | Procedure | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acibadem International patient guide | Published 3 September 2026, updated 12 September 2026 | Rhinoplasty | €3,650–5,600 |
| Acibadem International patient guide | Same | Septorhinoplasty | €3,900–7,150 |
| Bookimed (marketplace) | Header "Updated: August 6, 2024"; footer "Last updated: October, 2026" | Open rhinoplasty | $2,500–4,500 "all-inclusive" |
| Bookimed | Same | Closed or tip rhinoplasty | $1,800–3,000 |
| Bookimed | Same | Revision | $3,500–6,000 |

Acibadem says its figures come from the Q3 2026 edition of the Turkey Medical Price Index, that they are indicative, and that a personal quote follows medical review. Bookimed's page shows two different dates, so we cannot say precisely when its numbers were set. It also lists clinic package examples from about $2,805 to $5,217, with different inclusions, and notes that some patient reviews mention extra charges for medication or an echocardiogram.

Both are commercial pages. Acibadem is a hospital group describing its own market; Bookimed earns from patient referrals. Read them as marketing-adjacent price indications.

## What is published for Albania?

For Albania, the published and reported figures are mostly in the €2,000–4,500 range for primary rhinoplasty, with revision at €3,800–5,500 on one hospital's site. Italian press reported €1,800–3,500 in 2025. Details and dates are in [prices in 2026](/cost/prices-2026/).

## How do the two compare?

Compared directly, the ranges overlap, and the lowest Turkish package in the Bookimed list (about $1,800 for closed or tip rhinoplasty) sits below the lowest hospital price we found in Albania. At the top, Turkish septorhinoplasty is listed up to €7,150, above Albanian figures.

| | Albania (published, mixed sources) | Turkey (Acibadem, Bookimed) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary rhinoplasty | About €2,000–4,500 | €3,650–5,600 (Acibadem); $2,500–4,500 open, $1,800–3,000 closed or tip (Bookimed) |
| Septorhinoplasty | €3,200–4,200 (one hospital) | €3,900–7,150 (Acibadem) |
| Revision | €3,800–5,500 (one hospital) | $3,500–6,000 (Bookimed) |

Do not read this as "Albania is cheaper" or "Turkey is cheaper". Currencies differ (euros and dollars), the dates differ, and the Albanian figures come from two hospitals and press estimates while the Turkish figures come from a hospital group and a marketplace. The only safe conclusion is that you should ask for itemised written quotes from both countries.

## What matters besides price?

Besides price, five things decide the choice, and they favour neither country by default.

1. **The surgeon.** Check training, specialty and registration in the country where you will be operated on and, for visiting surgeons, in their home country. Many surgeons working in Tirana trained in Turkey or Italy and may visit rather than live in Albania. See [how to verify a surgeon](/surgeons/how-to-verify/).
2. **The setting.** A hospital or day hospital with an anaesthetist and overnight care is different from a small clinic. See [hospital or clinic](/clinics/hospital-or-clinic/).
3. **Verification.** In Albania we found 26 entities listing rhinoplasty or nearby services, and no public online register of licensed clinics or doctors was found, so verification takes more work. Whichever country you pick, check the register that applies there.
4. **Follow-up.** The NHS notes that overseas clinics may not provide follow-up treatment and advises asking before booking where follow-up appointments will be and how easily you could return for further surgery.
5. **Distance and flights.** Flight time and cost depend on where you live. The NHS advises avoiding flying for 7 to 10 days after facial procedures; your surgeon may advise differently, so plan a stay that fits.

## What about packages and hotels?

Packages in both countries often bundle hotel and transfers, and Turkish sources say so explicitly (Acibadem: hotel and transfers appear in most international packages and should be confirmed in writing; Bookimed lists transfers and online follow-up at 1 and 4 weeks). In Albania, one hospital says hotel and flights are extra. Whatever the country, compare complete totals. See [what a quote should include](/cost/whats-included/) and [hidden costs](/cost/hidden-costs/).

The NHS also advises being wary of surgery sold as a holiday, meeting the surgeon yourself rather than only sales staff, and not paying for a hospital or surgeon you have not seen or met.

## How should you decide?

Decide by requesting the same written quote from at least two providers in each country you are considering, and compare them line by line.

- Ask each for the operating surgeon's name, registration and rhinoplasty experience.
- Ask for the facility licence and the nights of stay included.
- Ask for the follow-up plan after you fly home, in writing.
- Ask for the revision terms.
- Add flights, hotel and a reserve for a return trip.

Use the [questions to ask a clinic](/clinics/questions-to-ask/) as a script.
