Most of your recovery happens at home, after the clinic stops seeing you. That is where overseas patients are most exposed, because the NHS notes that overseas clinics may not provide follow-up care to the same standard, and may have no healthcare professional near your home. This guide covers the daily care rules, how to set up local follow-up, and what to keep from your surgery.

## What daily care rules do the sources give?

The rules below come from Cleveland Clinic's rhinoplasty page. Where your surgeon's written instructions differ, follow your surgeon.

- **Sleep with your head elevated**, especially at night.
- **Use cool compresses (not ice)** on the cheeks or under the eyes.
- **Do not blow your nose.**
- **Avoid facial impact.**
- **Avoid pressure on the bridge from glasses.**
- **Keep out of the sun.** Cleveland Clinic advises minimising sun exposure.
- **No heavy lifting for four to six weeks.** Light exercise may begin within four to six weeks.
- **Take over-the-counter pain relievers** as your provider directs.

ASPS says your surgeon will give specific instructions on surgical-site care, medications, warning signs and follow-up, and that you should ask when you can resume normal activity and exercise. StatPearls lists avoiding activities that raise blood pressure as part of nursing advice.

If you smoke, MedlinePlus advises stopping at least 2 to 3 weeks before and after surgery. MedlinePlus says to stop blood-thinning medicines before surgery using the surgeon's list.

## When do the splint and stitches come out?

Splints and stitches are usually dealt with in the first one to two weeks, which is often before you leave Albania. See the [recovery day by day](/recovery/day-by-day/) guide. ASPS says the dressing is typically removed at one week along with any stitches. If something remains in place when you fly home, ask exactly who will remove it and when.

## How do you set up local follow-up?

Arrange it before you travel, not after a problem starts. The NHS advises asking what happens if complications occur, where follow-up appointments will take place, and how easily you could return for more surgery. ASPS warns that finding a qualified surgeon to treat a complication or perform a revision can be difficult, and that local doctors may not know which techniques the original surgeon used.

Practical steps:

1. **Tell your own doctor** (GP or family doctor) before you go. Ask whether they will check you after you return.
2. **Find a nose surgeon at home** whom you could see if there is a problem. A plastic surgeon or an ENT surgeon can assess a nose. See [plastic surgeon or ENT surgeon](/surgeons/plastic-surgeon-or-ent/). If you live in Italy, a surgeon in Italy can do this check.
3. **Ask the clinic in writing** whether it offers remote follow-up (photos, video call) and how fast it replies.
4. **Agree dates.** Ask for a check by the operating surgeon before you leave, and a plan for contact at about one week and one month after you are home.

## What records should you keep?

Keep everything that describes what was done to your nose. Ask the clinic before you leave for:

- an operation note or discharge summary that says which technique was used (open or closed, grafts, where cartilage came from, whether the septum was touched);
- the names and licence numbers of the surgeon and anaesthetist;
- a list of medicines you were given;
- your before and after photos;
- the clinic's written aftercare and revision terms;
- your invoice and receipt.

These matter because ASPS says local doctors may not know the original techniques. A clear note saves time if you need an urgent check or a [revision](/procedures/revision-rhinoplasty/).

## What can you expect at home?

Expect swelling and a slowly changing nose for months. Cleveland Clinic says about 90% of swelling is gone after three months, and full results usually appear after a year. Read [swelling month by month](/recovery/swelling-month-by-month/) so you do not judge the nose too early. Bruising fades in a week or two, says ASPS. A numb tip is common, says MedlinePlus.

Bleeding is often mild. StatPearls says it usually resolves with head elevation and nasal decongestant sprays. Use a spray only if your surgeon has told you to.

## When should you contact someone?

Contact your operating surgeon first, and use emergency services for severe signs. Cleveland Clinic says to contact your provider for difficulty breathing, nausea or vomiting, signs of infection such as fever or yellow discharge, severe pain and uncontrolled nosebleeds. The NHS lists excessive bleeding, or a nosebleed lasting longer than 10 to 15 minutes, as a reason to go to A&E. Read [problems after you get home](/safety/problems-after-you-get-home/) for the full list and what to do.

If you want to judge the result, wait. StatPearls says revision is generally delayed 6 to 12 months.

## How can a companion or family member help?

A person at home can do several jobs for you in the first week: remind you to keep your head raised, watch for warning signs, take photos for the clinic, and drive you if you need urgent care. Cleveland Clinic advises having someone stay with you overnight after surgery. Show them the list of emergency signs in problems after you get home before you travel, and give them the surgeon's contact details and your records. It helps that someone other than you knows where the documents are, particularly if you are drowsy from medicines.
