The sources we reviewed support a stay of at least 7 to 10 days after rhinoplasty, and up to about two weeks for some patients. No source gives a stay specific to Albania, so this guide builds the range from what is known about the splint, the first check and flying. The operating surgeon sets your date.

## How long should you stay?

Plan for roughly 7 to 14 days after surgery, and let the surgeon narrow it. Three points support that range.

- **Flying.** The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) says to wait 7 to 10 days before flying after facial procedures, including rhinoplasty. The NHS gives the same range for facial procedures. See [when you can fly after rhinoplasty](/recovery/flying-after-rhinoplasty/).
- **The splint.** StatPearls says the cast, tape and splints are usually removed at 7 days. ASPS says the dressing is typically kept for one week. Cleveland Clinic and MedlinePlus say the splint stays one to two weeks.
- **Packing.** Cleveland Clinic says packing is usually removed within 24 to 48 hours, and MedlinePlus says in 3 to 5 days.

Complex cases, [revision rhinoplasty](/procedures/revision-rhinoplasty/), or extra procedures can take longer. MedlinePlus notes that complex cases may need a short hospital stay.

## What happens in the days after surgery?

The first week is for resting and the first check. Cleveland Clinic says pain should lessen within a few days to a week. ASPS says pain and swelling are most pronounced in the first week. Most people go home the same day as surgery, says MedlinePlus, so you will spend your first nights in a hotel or apartment rather than a ward. Read [where to stay while you recover](/travel/where-to-stay/).

A typical sequence, built from the sources:

1. Day of surgery: home or hotel with someone to stay overnight (Cleveland Clinic).
2. Days 1 to 2 or up to 5: packing out, if used.
3. About day 7: dressing and splint check and removal, and stitches out if needed (ASPS, StatPearls).
4. Days 7 to 10: surgeon's clearance to fly (ASPS, NHS).

Your own schedule may differ. See the [recovery day by day](/recovery/day-by-day/) guide.

## Is it realistic to be a tourist?

No, according to the NHS. It calls holidaying straight after surgery "unrealistic" and says it can increase the risk of things going wrong. It advises rest and avoiding alcohol, sunbathing, sightseeing and swimming. ASPS gives a similar list for people who travel for surgery, and adds long walking or bus tours, water sports and exercise.

Cleveland Clinic gives more guidance for the first weeks: keep your head elevated, avoid facial impact and pressure on the bridge from glasses, minimise sun exposure and do not blow your nose. A quiet, close-by hotel with an easy route to the clinic fits these needs better than a sightseeing plan.

## What should you arrange with the clinic?

Arrange the dates and the follow-up in writing before you book flights. The NHS advises asking what happens if complications occur, where follow-up appointments will take place, and how easily you could return for more surgery. It also says you should have two consultations with the surgeon before surgery, and to avoid consultations with sales staff only. For an itemised list of what a quote should cover, read [what a rhinoplasty quote should include](/cost/whats-included/).

Ask these questions:

- On which day will the operating surgeon check me, and on which day will the splint come off?
- Who is available in an emergency after hours, and in which language?
- What is the earliest date you would let me fly, and what would change it?
- Do you give me written aftercare instructions and an operation note to take home?
- Are check-ups included in the price, and is there a charge if I must stay extra nights?

Our questions to ask a clinic page has a longer list.

## How should you book flights and hotel?

Book refundable or changeable flights and a hotel you can extend. If you book the minimum, you pay to change the dates or feel pressure to leave before you are ready. Insurance matters too: the NHS says travel insurance normally does not cover problems arising from surgery, and advises checking what the clinic or surgeon's insurance covers and planning how you would get home in an emergency. See planning your trip.

## When should you not leave?

Do not leave while you have a problem the surgeon has not looked at. That includes bleeding that is more than mild, a fever, severe pain that is getting worse, or trouble breathing. Cleveland Clinic lists difficulty breathing, nausea or vomiting, signs of infection such as fever or yellow discharge, severe pain and uncontrolled nosebleeds as reasons to contact your provider. If the sign is severe, use the emergency number: 127 for an ambulance in Albania, or 112 in EU countries.

## What if recovery goes more slowly?

Be ready to stay a few extra days. Bruising and swelling vary from person to person, and sources give ranges, not fixed dates. ASPS says bruising usually lasts a week or two and Cleveland Clinic says swelling can be uneven in the first weeks. If your surgeon wants to see you again, or the splint needs to stay longer, the extra nights are part of the plan. Ask the clinic in advance what extra nights cost and whether a follow-up visit is included in the price, so that a short delay does not become a financial worry. Also check that your work, childcare and travel insurance can cope with a flexible return date.
